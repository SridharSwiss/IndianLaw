# Protocol: Indian Patent Application Filing

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-001
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Filing Procedures
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive protocol for filing patent applications with the Indian Patent Office (IPO), covering ordinary applications, convention applications, PCT national phase entries, and divisional applications under the Patents Act, 1970 and Patents Rules, 2003 (as amended 2024).

---

## Activation Triggers

- User needs to file a patent application in India
- Questions about patent filing requirements, forms, or deadlines
- PCT national phase entry into India
- Divisional application filing strategy
- Startup/small entity fee benefits inquiry

---

## Prerequisites

- Invention disclosure or draft specification
- Inventor and applicant details
- Priority document (if claiming convention priority)
- Power of Attorney (Form 26) if using patent agent
- Entity status determination (natural person/startup/small entity/large entity)

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Determine Application Type

1. **Ordinary Application**: No foreign priority claim
2. **Convention Application**: Claiming priority from Paris Convention country (within 12 months)
3. **PCT National Phase**: Entry from international application (within 31 months + 6 months extension)
4. **Divisional Application**: Splitting from parent application under Section 16

### Step 2: Prepare Required Documents

#### For All Applications:
- **Form 1**: Application for grant of patent
- **Form 2**: Complete/Provisional Specification
- **Form 3**: Statement and undertaking regarding foreign applications (Section 8)
- **Form 5**: Declaration of inventorship
- **Drawings** (if applicable)

#### Additional for Convention/PCT:
- Priority document (certified copy)
- Verified English translation (if not in English)
- Form 26: Power of Attorney

### Step 3: Determine Appropriate Patent Office

| Applicant Location | Patent Office |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Delhi, HP, J&K, Punjab, Rajasthan, UP, Haryana, Uttarakhand | Delhi |
| Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP, Goa, Chhattisgarh | Mumbai |
| West Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, NE States | Kolkata |
| Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, AP, Telangana, Puducherry | Chennai |

### Step 4: Calculate and Pay Fees

#### Filing Fees (Electronic Filing):
| Entity Type | Complete Spec | Provisional Spec |
|-------------|---------------|------------------|
| Natural Person/Startup | ₹1,600 | ₹1,600 |
| Small Entity | ₹4,000 | ₹4,000 |
| Large Entity | ₹8,000 | ₹8,000 |

**Note**: 10% additional for physical filing

### Step 5: File Application

1. Access IPO e-filing portal: https://ipindiaonline.gov.in
2. Create/login to account
3. Upload all documents
4. Pay fees online
5. Download filing receipt with application number

### Step 6: Post-Filing Compliance

1. **Form 3 Updates**: File within 6 months of Indian filing, then updated Form 3 within 3 months of First Examination Report (FER)
2. **Request for Examination (Form 18)**: Within 31 months (for applications filed after March 15, 2024)
3. **Publication**: Automatic at 18 months, or early publication via Form 9

---

## Timeline Reference

| Milestone | Deadline |
|-----------|----------|
| PCT National Phase Entry | 31 months from priority (extendable +6 months) |
| Convention Application | 12 months from first filing |
| Provisional to Complete | 12 months from provisional filing |
| Request for Examination | 31 months (post-March 2024 filings) |
| Response to FER | 6 months (extendable +3 months) |

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Correct application type selected
- [ ] All required forms completed accurately
- [ ] Specification meets disclosure requirements (Section 10)
- [ ] Claims are clear, succinct, and fairly based on specification
- [ ] Priority document filed (if applicable)
- [ ] Form 3 compliance checked
- [ ] Correct entity status declared (Form 28)
- [ ] Fees calculated and paid correctly
- [ ] E-filing receipt obtained

---

## Common Errors to Avoid

1. **Missing Form 3**: Can lead to opposition/revocation grounds
2. **Incorrect priority claim**: Verify dates and application numbers
3. **Late RFE filing**: Application deemed withdrawn
4. **Wrong entity status**: Can invalidate fee benefits
5. **Incomplete specification**: Insufficient disclosure objections

# Protocol: Patent Specification Drafting

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-002
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Specification Drafting
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guidelines for drafting patent specifications (provisional and complete) under Indian patent law, ensuring compliance with Section 10 disclosure requirements and optimizing claim scope for protection.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Drafting a new patent specification
- Converting provisional to complete specification
- Amending claims during prosecution
- Claim drafting strategy questions
- Disclosure sufficiency concerns

---

## Prerequisites

- Complete invention disclosure from inventor
- Prior art search results
- Understanding of commercial embodiments
- Knowledge of competitive landscape

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Section 10 Requirements

The specification must:
1. **Fully and particularly describe** the invention and its operation
2. **Disclose the best method** of performing the invention known to applicant
3. **End with claims** defining scope of protection
4. Claims must be **clear, succinct, and fairly based** on the specification

### Step 2: Structure the Specification

#### Title
- Concise, descriptive of invention
- Match with independent claim preamble
- Avoid marketing language

#### Field of Invention
- Technical field to which invention pertains
- Sets context for novelty/inventive step analysis

#### Background/Prior Art
- Describe existing solutions objectively
- Identify technical problems/shortcomings
- DO NOT admit prior art that isn't documented

#### Summary of Invention
- Brief description of solution
- Key technical advantages
- Reference to all independent claims

#### Detailed Description
- Multiple embodiments (preferred and alternatives)
- Enable person skilled in art (POSITA) to practice
- Describe all figures/drawings
- Include examples where applicable

#### Claims
- Independent claims: Broadest scope
- Dependent claims: Specific features, fallback positions

### Step 3: Claim Drafting Best Practices

#### Independent Claim Structure
```
A [preamble - what the invention is],
comprising/characterized by:
[body - essential elements and their relationships]
```

#### Avoid Two-Part Claims When Possible
- Indian law doesn't mandate two-part format
- IPO examiners may request amendment to two-part
- Two-part format can limit claim interpretation

#### Claim Types to Include
1. **Product/Apparatus claims**: Physical structure
2. **Method/Process claims**: Steps to achieve result
3. **System claims**: Multiple components working together
4. **Use claims**: Specific applications (where applicable)

### Step 4: Special Considerations by Technology

#### Computer-Related Inventions (CRIs)
- Focus on **technical effect** (per 2025 CRI Guidelines)
- Avoid claiming "computer program per se"
- Include hardware elements in system claims
- Describe algorithm implementation, not just algorithm

**Example (Allowable):**
"A system for preventing unauthorized access comprising:
- a processor configured to execute...
- a memory storing instructions that...
- resulting in reduced latency and improved security"

**Example (Not Allowable):**
"A method of pricing comprising calculating... [pure business method]"

#### Biotechnology/Pharmaceuticals
- Avoid Section 3(d) pitfalls (mere new forms)
- Demonstrate **enhanced therapeutic efficacy**
- Include experimental data supporting efficacy
- Markush claims for chemical variations

#### Mechanical Inventions
- Detailed structural descriptions
- Multiple drawing views
- Dimensional relationships where critical

### Step 5: Disclosure Best Practices

1. **Enablement**: Can POSITA make and use without undue experimentation?
2. **Written Description**: Full support for all claims
3. **Best Mode**: Disclose inventor's preferred embodiment
4. **Examples**: Include working examples, especially for chemistry/biotech

### Step 6: Drawings

- **Rule 15**: Drawings must be in permanent ink, black lines
- Scale: Suitable for reproduction at 2/3 reduction
- Reference numerals consistent with specification
- Flow charts for method claims
- Block diagrams for system claims

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Title matches independent claim preamble
- [ ] Background doesn't admit unnecessary prior art
- [ ] All elements in claims described in specification
- [ ] Claims are clear and succinct
- [ ] Claims fairly based on specification
- [ ] Best mode disclosed
- [ ] Multiple embodiments included
- [ ] Drawings properly numbered and referenced
- [ ] No prohibited subject matter (Section 3)
- [ ] CRI claims show technical effect
- [ ] Pharma claims avoid Section 3(d)

---

## Common Drafting Pitfalls

1. **Over-narrow claims**: Lost commercial coverage
2. **Over-broad claims**: Invalid over prior art
3. **Insufficient disclosure**: Grounds for revocation
4. **Best mode not disclosed**: Revocation ground (Section 64)
5. **Claims not fairly based**: Rejection during prosecution
6. **Pure algorithm/business method claims**: Section 3(k) rejection

---

## Claim Hierarchy Example

```
Claim 1 (Independent - Broadest):
A method for [technical result] comprising:
  step a);
  step b);
  step c).

Claim 2 (Dependent):
The method of claim 1, wherein step a) includes [specific feature].

Claim 3 (Dependent):
The method of claim 1 or 2, further comprising step d).

Claim 4 (Independent - Apparatus):
A system for implementing the method of claim 1, comprising:
  processor;
  memory;
  [specific components].
```

# Protocol: Patent Examination and Prosecution

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-003
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Prosecution
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Guide the patent prosecution process from Request for Examination through grant, including responding to examination reports, amendments, hearings, and expedited examination procedures.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Filing Request for Examination
- Responding to First Examination Report (FER)
- Amending claims during prosecution
- Preparing for hearing before Controller
- Seeking expedited examination

---

## Prerequisites

- Filed patent application with application number
- Understanding of prior art landscape
- Claim scope strategy defined
- Timeline awareness for deadlines

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: File Request for Examination (RFE)

#### Timeline (Post-March 15, 2024):
- **Deadline**: 31 months from priority/filing date (whichever earlier)
- **Extension**: Up to 6 months with fee
- **Consequence of missing**: Application deemed withdrawn

#### Form and Fees:
| Entity Type | Form 18 Fee (E-filing) |
|-------------|------------------------|
| Natural Person/Startup | ₹4,000 |
| Small Entity | ₹10,000 |
| Large Entity | ₹20,000 |

### Step 2: Expedited Examination Options

#### Eligible Applicants (Rule 24C):
1. **Startups** (DPIIT recognized)
2. **Small Entities** (MSME Act definition)
3. **Female applicants** (natural persons)
4. **Government/public institutions**
5. **Applicants under PPH** (currently with Japan only)
6. **Green/eco-friendly inventions**

#### Expedited Examination Process:
1. File Form 18A with eligibility documents
2. Pay expedited examination fee
3. FER issued within **105 days** of acceptance
4. Target grant: **12-18 months** vs. 3-5 years normal

### Step 3: Receive and Analyze FER

#### FER Contains:
- Prior art citations
- Objections under Sections 3 (non-patentable subject matter)
- Novelty objections (Section 2(1)(l))
- Inventive step objections (Section 2(1)(ja))
- Sufficiency of disclosure objections (Section 10)
- Unity of invention objections (Section 10(5))
- Formal objections

#### Key Analysis Points:
1. Identify most damaging prior art
2. Assess claim differentiation options
3. Evaluate amendment strategy
4. Consider divisional applications if unity issue

### Step 4: Prepare Response to FER

#### Response Deadline:
- **6 months** from FER date
- **Extension**: 3 months (request can be filed during extension period per 2024 Rules)

#### Response Components:

**1. Arguments (Mandatory):**
- Address each objection point-by-point
- Distinguish prior art
- Support patentability arguments with evidence

**2. Claim Amendments (If Needed):**
- Narrow claims to overcome prior art
- Add features from dependent claims
- Ensure amendments don't add new matter

**3. Specification Amendments (If Needed):**
- Clarify description
- Fix inconsistencies
- Cannot add new matter

**4. Declaration/Evidence (If Applicable):**
- Technical expert declarations
- Comparative data
- Commercial success evidence

### Step 5: Form 3 Compliance

Per 2024 Rules, updated Form 3 required within **3 months of FER** containing:
- Status of all corresponding foreign applications
- Prior art cited in foreign prosecution
- Examination results from other offices

### Step 6: Subsequent Examination Reports

If Controller not satisfied:
1. Second examination report issued
2. Additional arguments/amendments filed
3. Process continues until:
   - Grant
   - Final rejection
   - Abandonment

### Step 7: Hearing Before Controller

#### When Hearing Occurs:
- Controller intends to refuse application
- Complex technical/legal issues
- Applicant requests hearing

#### Hearing Preparation:
1. Prepare written submissions in advance
2. Organize technical evidence
3. Anticipate Controller questions
4. Consider technical expert attendance
5. Prepare claim amendments as backup

#### Hearing Fee (2024 Rules):
₹7,500 - must be paid with notice of intention to attend

### Step 8: Grant of Patent

Upon acceptance:
1. Application published for opposition window
2. If no opposition, patent granted
3. Certificate issued with claims, drawings
4. Patent valid for 20 years from filing date

---

## Amendment Best Practices

### During Prosecution:
- Amendments must not broaden scope
- Must have support in originally filed specification
- No new matter
- Track all changes clearly

### Amendment Strategy:
1. **Fallback positions**: Use dependent claim features
2. **Characterization**: Add distinguishing features
3. **Functional limitations**: Narrow scope without losing coverage
4. **Method vs. product**: Consider format changes

---

## Timeline Management

| Event | Deadline | Extension Available |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| RFE Filing | 31 months | +6 months with fee |
| Response to FER | 6 months | +3 months |
| Form 3 Update | 3 months from FER | No |
| Hearing Attendance Notice | Before hearing | No |

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] RFE filed within deadline
- [ ] All FER objections addressed
- [ ] Claim amendments don't add new matter
- [ ] Prior art distinguished with specificity
- [ ] Form 3 updated with foreign prosecution info
- [ ] Response filed within deadline
- [ ] Hearing preparation complete (if applicable)

---

## Common Prosecution Pitfalls

1. **Missing deadlines**: No substantive extensions available
2. **Insufficient arguments**: Generic responses rejected
3. **Incomplete Form 3**: Opposition/revocation ground
4. **New matter in amendments**: Rejected
5. **Overly aggressive narrowing**: Commercial coverage lost

# Protocol: PCT National Phase Entry in India

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-004
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: International Filing
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for entering the national phase in India from an international (PCT) application, including requirements, deadlines, documentation, and strategic considerations.

---

## Activation Triggers

- PCT application approaching national phase deadline
- Strategy for India national phase entry
- Document requirements for PCT-NP filing
- Deadline calculations and extensions
- Amendment options at national phase entry

---

## Prerequisites

- International application filed under PCT
- International publication (or filing receipt)
- Priority document (if claiming priority)
- Assignment documents (if applicant different from inventor)
- Understanding of Indian patentability requirements

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Calculate Entry Deadline

#### Standard Deadline:
- **31 months** from priority date (or international filing date if no priority)

#### Extension Available:
- Up to **6 months additional** with extension fee
- Total possible: **37 months** from priority date

#### Fee for Extension:
| Entity Type | Extension Fee (per month) |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Natural Person/Startup | ₹10,000 |
| Small Entity | ₹25,000 |
| Large Entity | ₹50,000 |

### Step 2: Prepare Required Documents

#### Mandatory Documents:

1. **Form 1** - Application for grant of patent
   - Mark as "National Phase Application"
   - Include international application number
   - Include international filing date
   - Include international publication number

2. **Complete Specification** (Form 2)
   - Copy of PCT specification in English
   - Or verified English translation if filed in other language

3. **Form 3** - Statement under Section 8
   - Details of all foreign applications
   - Must be filed within 6 months of national phase entry

4. **Form 5** - Declaration of inventorship
   - If not already on record from PCT

5. **Drawings** (if any)
   - As published with PCT application

#### Additional Documents (If Applicable):

6. **Power of Attorney (Form 26)**
   - If filing through Indian patent agent
   - Scanned copy acceptable initially
   - Original within 15 days if requested

7. **Assignment Deed**
   - If applicant is not the inventor
   - Notarized copy required
   - Must be filed within 6 months

8. **Priority Document**
   - Not required if PCT published in English
   - Required with verified translation if PCT in other language

9. **Form 28** - Entity Status Declaration
   - If claiming startup/small entity benefits

### Step 3: Language Requirements

| PCT Language | India Requirement |
|--------------|-------------------|
| English | File as-is |
| Other | Verified English translation required |

**Translation Deadline**:
- At filing, or within 3 months of IPO invitation
- Extension up to 6 months with fee available

### Step 4: Calculate and Pay Fees

#### National Phase Entry Fees (E-filing):
| Fee Type | Natural Person/Startup | Small Entity | Large Entity |
|----------|----------------------|--------------|--------------|
| Filing Fee | ₹1,600 | ₹4,000 | ₹8,000 |
| Per additional claim (>10) | ₹160 | ₹400 | ₹800 |
| Per page specification (>30) | ₹160 | ₹400 | ₹800 |
| Per additional sheet drawing (>10) | ₹160 | ₹400 | ₹800 |

### Step 5: File National Phase Application

1. Access IPO e-filing portal
2. Select "National Phase Application" option
3. Enter international application details
4. Upload all required documents
5. Pay applicable fees
6. Obtain Indian application number

### Step 6: Amendments at National Phase Entry

#### What CAN be Done:
- Delete claims (cannot amend)
- Reflect amendments already published on WIPO
- Correct obvious errors
- Submit translations

#### What CANNOT be Done:
- Amend claim text (no broadening or narrowing)
- Add new claims
- Change applicant/inventor details not on WIPO record

**Note**: Amendments possible AFTER entry during prosecution

### Step 7: Post-Entry Requirements

1. **Request for Examination (Form 18)**
   - Deadline: 31 months from priority date
   - For applications entered before March 15, 2024: 48 months

2. **Form 3 Updates**
   - Within 6 months of entry
   - Updated Form 3 within 3 months of FER

3. **Expedited Examination**
   - Available via Form 18A if eligible

---

## Strategic Considerations

### Timing Entry:
- **Early entry**: Faster prosecution, earlier grant
- **Late entry**: More time to assess commercial value
- **Extension**: Buy additional time if needed

### Claim Strategy:
- Review PCT search report and written opinion
- Consider amendments needed for India-specific issues
- Plan divisional applications if unity objection likely

### Entity Status:
- Claim startup/small entity status at entry
- Significant fee savings throughout patent life

# Protocol: Divisional Patent Applications

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-005
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Application Strategy
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for filing and prosecuting divisional patent applications under Section 16 of the Patents Act, 1970, including the significant changes from the 2024 Amendment Rules and the Syngenta decision.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Unity of invention objection in examination report
- Desire to protect multiple inventions disclosed in parent
- Strategic portfolio expansion
- Continuation-style prosecution strategy

---

## Prerequisites

- Pending parent application (not yet granted)
- Multiple inventions disclosed in provisional or complete specification
- Understanding of claim scope strategy

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Legal Basis

#### Section 16 - Divisional Applications:
"A person who has made an application for a patent...may, at any time before the grant of the patent, if he so desires, or with a view to remedy the objection raised by the Controller on the ground that the claims of the complete specification relate to more than one invention, file a further application..."

#### Key 2024 Amendment (Rule 13(2A)):
Applicants may file one or more further applications under Section 16, including for an invention disclosed in:
- The **provisional specification**
- The **complete specification**
- A **further divisional application**

This aligns with the Delhi High Court **Syngenta decision (October 2023)**.

### Step 2: Determine Filing Basis

#### Traditional Basis (Pre-2024):
- Unity objection from Controller
- Claims relate to more than one invention

#### Voluntary Filing (Post-2024):
- **Suo moto** filing without unity objection
- Based on any invention disclosed in:
  - Provisional specification
  - Complete specification description
  - Previously filed divisional

### Step 3: Timing Requirements

| Condition | Deadline |
|-----------|----------|
| Before parent grant | Mandatory - cannot file after parent grants |
| Voluntary filing | Any time before parent grant |
| Controller-directed | Per direction in examination report |

**Critical**: Once parent patent is granted, divisional filing window closes.

### Step 4: Prepare Divisional Application

#### Documents Required:
1. **Form 1** - Mark as divisional application
   - Reference parent application number
   - Reference filing date of parent
2. **Form 2** - Complete specification
   - New claims directed to divisional invention
   - Description can be same as parent or modified
3. **Form 3** - Statement under Section 8
4. **Form 5** - Declaration of inventorship

#### Claim Requirements:
- Claims must relate to invention disclosed in parent
- Must be distinct from claims in parent
- Cannot broaden beyond parent disclosure
- No new matter permitted

### Step 5: Filing and Priority

#### Effective Filing Date:
- Divisional deemed filed on **parent's filing date**
- Preserves all priority benefits of parent

#### Priority Claim:
- Inherits priority from parent automatically
- Same convention priority (if any)

### Step 6: Prosecution of Divisional

1. **Independent prosecution**: Separate from parent
2. **Own RFE deadline**: 31 months from original priority
3. **Own examination**: May receive different prior art
4. **Own grant timeline**: Independent of parent

### Step 7: Strategic Uses

#### 1. Portfolio Expansion
- Extract multiple patents from single disclosure
- Cover different aspects of same innovation

#### 2. Continuation-Style Strategy
- Similar to US continuation practice (per 2024 Rules)
- File divisionals to continue prosecution
- Pursue alternative claim scope

#### 3. Design-Around Protection
- Cover competitor variations
- Block design-arounds of parent claims

#### 4. Technology Licensing
- Separate patents for different licensees
- Flexibility in portfolio management

---

## Comparison with US Continuation Practice

| Feature | India Divisional (Post-2024) | US Continuation |
|---------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| Basis | Distinct invention disclosed | Same invention |
| New Matter | Not permitted | Not permitted |
| Timing | Before parent grant | Before parent issues |
| Voluntary Filing | Yes | Yes |
| Claim Overlap | Must be distinct | Can overlap |

**Note**: India still doesn't have true continuation applications. Divisionals must cover **distinct** inventions from parent claims.

---

## Key Case Law

### Syngenta Ltd. v. Controller of Patents (Delhi HC, October 2023)

- No longer good law

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-006
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law

**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for filing and prosecuting pre-grant oppositions under Section 25(1) of the Patents Act, 1970, including the significant changes from the 2024 Amendment Rules introducing prima facie review.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Competitor patent application threatens business
- Prior art discovered that invalidates pending application
- Public interest grounds for opposing patent grant
- Defensive patent strategy

---

## Prerequisites

- Published patent application (post 18 months or early publication)
- Identified grounds for opposition under Section 25(1)
- Evidence supporting opposition grounds
- Application not yet granted

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Verify Application Status

Before filing opposition:
1. Check application is **published** (search on InPASS)
2. Confirm application is **not yet granted**
3. Verify examination report issued (timing consideration)

**Timeline Window**: After publication, before grant

### Step 2: Identify Opposition Grounds (Section 25(1))

| Ground | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| (a) | Wrongfully obtained from opponent or predecessor |
| (b) | Published before priority date (anticipation) |
| (c) | Publicly known/used in India before priority date |
| (d) | Obvious in view of prior art |
| (e) | Not an invention under the Act |
| (f) | Non-patentable under Section 3 |
| (g) | Insufficient description |
| (h) | Non-disclosure of information under Section 8 |
| (i) | Convention application filed after 12 months |
| (j) | Not entitled to claim priority date |
| (k) | Claims not fairly based on matter disclosed |

### Step 3: Prepare Opposition Representation

#### Required Components:

1. **Written Statement of Opposition**
   - Detailed grounds with specificity
   - Reference to each claim opposed
   - Prima facie case establishment

2. **Evidence in Support**
   - Prior art documents
   - Expert declarations (if applicable)
   - Proof of prior use/knowledge
   - Section 8 non-compliance evidence

3. **Form and Fee**
   - Pre-grant opposition fee (2024 Rules):
     - ₹20,000 (all entities)
   - No prescribed form number

### Step 4: Prima Facie Review (NEW - 2024 Rules)

#### Rule 55(3) Requirement:
Controller must first assess whether representation discloses a **prima facie case** for opposition.

#### If Prima Facie Case Found:
1. Opposition accepted on record
2. Applicant notified
3. Application proceeds under **expedited examination** (Rule 24C)
4. Full opposition procedure initiated

#### If Prima Facie Case NOT Found:
1. Notice of rejection issued under Rule 55(3)
2. Opponent may request hearing
3. If no hearing requested, opposition dismissed
4. **Reasoned order** must be provided for refusal

### Step 5: Opposition Procedure (Post-Acceptance)

#### Timeline After Prima Facie Acceptance:

| Event | Timeline |
|-------|----------|
| Notice to applicant | Controller issues notice |
| Applicant's reply | Within prescribed period |
| Evidence submission | As directed by Controller |
| Hearing (if requested) | Scheduled by Controller |
| Controller's decision | After hearing/submissions |

### Step 6: Hearing Procedures

Per 2024 Rules, hearing procedures from post-grant opposition (Rules 62(2)-(4)) now apply to pre-grant:

1. **Notice of Hearing**: Issued to both parties
2. **Hearing Fee**: ₹7,500 (must be paid to attend)
3. **Written Submissions**: Filed before hearing
4. **Oral Arguments**: Both parties may present
5. **Decision**: Controller issues reasoned order

### Step 7: Possible Outcomes

1. **Opposition Succeeds**: Application refused
2. **Opposition Partially Succeeds**: Claims amended/deleted
3. **Opposition Fails**: Application proceeds to grant
4. **Opposition Dismissed at Prima Facie**: No further action

---

## 2024 Rule Changes Summary

| Aspect | Before 2024 | After 2024 |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| Prima Facie Filter | None | Mandatory under Rule 55(3) |
| Fee | None | ₹20,000 |
| Hearing Fee | None | ₹7,500 |
| Expedited Examination | No | Yes, if opposition accepted |
| Reasoned Order | Not required | Required for prima facie refusal |

- **Early**: Maximum time to gather evidence
- **After FER**: Leverage examiner's objections
- **Before grant imminent**: Last opportunity

- Strong documentary prior art preferred

- Filing fee: ₹20,000
- Hearing fee: ₹7,500 (if attending)
- Attorney fees
- Time investment
- vs. Risk of competitor patent grant

# Protocol: Post-Grant Opposition

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-007
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Opposition Proceedings
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for filing and prosecuting post-grant oppositions under Section 25(2) of the Patents Act, 1970, including Opposition Board procedures and timeline management.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Competitor patent granted threatens business
- Prior art discovered invalidating granted patent
- Alternative to revocation petition before High Court
- Public interest grounds for challenging patent

---

## Prerequisites

- Granted Indian patent (published in Patent Journal)
- "Person interested" status (locus standi)
- Identified grounds for opposition under Section 25(2)
- Evidence supporting opposition grounds
- Within 12-month window from grant publication

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Verify Eligibility

#### Standing Requirement ("Person Interested"):
Must establish:
- Engaged in same field of technology, OR
- Research in relevant field, OR
- Commercially affected by patent, OR
- Public interest stake

#### Timeline Requirement:
- **Within 12 months** from date of grant publication in Patent Journal
- Publication occurs after certificate issuance

### Step 2: Identify Opposition Grounds (Section 25(2))

Same grounds as pre-grant opposition:

| Ground | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| (a) | Wrongfully obtained from opponent |
| (b) | Published before priority date (anticipation) |
| (c) | Publicly known/used in India before priority |
| (d) | Obvious in view of prior art |
| (e) | Not an invention under the Act |
| (f) | Non-patentable under Section 3 |
| (g) | Insufficient description |
| (h) | Non-disclosure under Section 8 |
| (i) | Convention application beyond 12 months |
| (j) | Not entitled to claim priority |
| (k) | Claims not fairly based on disclosure |

### Step 3: File Notice of Opposition

#### Required Documents:

1. **Form 7** - Notice of Opposition
2. **Written Statement of Opposition**
   - Detailed grounds with specificity
   - Reference to each claim opposed
   - Evidence list
3. **Evidence in Support**
   - Prior art documents
   - Expert declarations
   - Proof of standing
4. **Fee Payment**
   - ₹40,000 (large entities)
   - ₹10,000 (small entities/individuals)

#### Filing Location:
Same patent office where patent granted (territorial jurisdiction)

### Step 4: Notice to Patentee

Controller issues notice to patentee:
- Copy of opposition notice
- Request for reply
- Timeline for response

### Step 5: Constitution of Opposition Board

#### Board Composition:
- Three members
- Technical and legal expertise
- Independent assessment

#### Board's Role:
1. Examine all documents submitted
2. Review evidence from both parties
3. Submit recommendations to Controller
4. Timeline: **2 months** (per 2024 Rules, reduced from 3)

### Step 6: Evidence Submission

#### Opponent's Evidence:
- Filed with notice of opposition
- Additional evidence with leave of Controller

#### Patentee's Reply Evidence:
- Filed within prescribed timeline
- Counter-evidence addressing opposition

### Step 7: Hearing

1. **Notice of Hearing**: To both parties
2. **Hearing Fee**: ₹7,500 (2024 Rules)
3. **Pre-Hearing Submissions**: Written arguments
4. **Oral Hearing**: Both parties present case
5. **Controller's Decision**: Based on Board recommendation

### Step 8: Controller's Decision

Possible outcomes:
1. **Opposition Upheld**: Patent revoked (fully or partially)
2. **Opposition Rejected**: Patent maintained
3. **Patent Amended**: Claims modified to overcome objection

---

## Timeline Summary

| Event | Deadline |
|-------|----------|
| Notice of Opposition | 12 months from grant publication |
| Patentee's Reply | As directed by Controller |
| Opposition Board Report | 2 months (2024 Rules) |
| Hearing | Scheduled by Controller |
| Controller's Decision | After hearing |

| Appeal | Limited | To High Court |

### When to Use Post-Grant Opposition:
- Missed pre-grant window
- Additional prior art discovered after grant

- Expert declarations strengthen technical arguments

- Allows time for complete evidence gathering
- Strategic timing with business considerations

#### If Opposition Rejected:

# Protocol: Patent Revocation

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-008
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Invalidity Proceedings
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for patent revocation proceedings under Section 64 of the Patents Act, 1970, before High Courts, including grounds, procedures, and strategic considerations post-IPAB abolition.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Challenge validity of competitor's granted patent
- Defense in infringement action (counterclaim)
- Post-grant opposition period expired
- Need for in rem invalidation effect
- Central Government initiated revocation

---

## Prerequisites

- Granted Indian patent to challenge
- Identified grounds under Section 64(1)
- Standing as "person interested" or Central Government
- Evidence supporting revocation grounds

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Jurisdictional Changes (Post-IPAB 2021)

#### Post-Tribunal Reforms Act, 2021:
- IPAB abolished effective April 4, 2021
- Revocation jurisdiction transferred to **High Courts**
- Primary High Courts: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai
- IP Divisions established in Delhi and Madras High Courts

#### Which High Court?
- Patent office where application filed (for original jurisdiction)
- Where cause of action arises (for revocation)

### Step 2: Identify Revocation Grounds (Section 64(1))

17 statutory grounds:

| Ground | Section | Description |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| (a) | 64(1)(a) | Wrongfully obtained |
| (b) | 64(1)(b) | Granted to non-entitled person |
| (c) | 64(1)(c) | Prior claim in valid earlier patent |
| (d) | 64(1)(d) | Invention not new |
| (e) | 64(1)(e) | Obvious/lack inventive step |
| (f) | 64(1)(f) | Not useful |
| (g) | 64(1)(g) | Insufficient description |
| (h) | 64(1)(h) | Not entitled to priority date |
| (i) | 64(1)(i) | Claims not fairly based |
| (j) | 64(1)(j) | Non-patentable under Section 3 |
| (k) | 64(1)(k) | Withheld information under Section 8 |
| (l) | 64(1)(l) | Non-compliance with Section 8 direction |
| (m) | 64(1)(m) | Patent obtained on false suggestion |
| (n) | 64(1)(n) | Undisclosed invention used by government |
| (o) | 64(1)(o) | Application should have been refused |
| (p) | 64(1)(p) | Against public order/morality |
| (q) | 64(1)(q) | Non-compliance at examination |

### Step 3: Filing Revocation Petition

#### Who Can File:
1. **Any person interested** (wide standing)
2. **Central Government** (public interest)
3. **Counterclaim in infringement suit** (defendant)

#### Required Documents:
1. Petition with grounds clearly stated
2. Supporting evidence/documents
3. Claim analysis showing invalidity
4. Prior art documents with translations
5. Expert affidavits (if technical complexity)

### Step 4: Procedure Before High Court

1. **Filing**: Petition filed with prescribed fees
2. **Notice**: Served on patentee
3. **Written Statement**: Patentee's response
4. **Evidence**: Documentary and expert evidence
5. **Arguments**: Oral hearing before judge
6. **Judgment**: Court's reasoned order

### Step 5: Counterclaim in Infringement Suit

#### Section 64(1) proviso:
When revocation sought by counterclaim:
- Filed in same court as infringement suit
- High Court with original jurisdiction

#### Strategic Advantage:
- Defensive strategy in infringement
- Validity tested in same proceeding
- If successful, patent revoked (not just invalidity finding)

### Step 6: Effect of Revocation

#### In Rem Effect:
- Patent removed from Register of Patents
- "As if never existed"
- Binding on all parties (not just litigants)
- Cannot be enforced against anyone

#### Contrast with Invalidity Defense:
- Section 107 defense only creates in personam effect
- Requires additional steps under Section 71(1) for register correction
- Only binds parties to that suit

---

## Revocation vs. Section 107 Invalidity Defense

| Aspect | Revocation (Sec 64) | Invalidity Defense (Sec 107) |
|--------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| Effect | In rem (everyone) | In personam (parties only) |
| Outcome | Patent removed from register | Patent remains on register |
| Post-decision | No further action | Rectification under Sec 71(1) needed |
| Standing | Person interested | Defendant in infringement |
| Timing | Any time (even after patent expiry) | During infringement suit |

### Key Case: Macleods v. Lilly (Delhi HC)
Court clarified scope differences between revocation and invalidity defense.

---

## Timing Considerations

#### No Limitation Period:
- Revocation petition can be filed even after patent expiry
- Delhi HC confirmed in recent ruling
- Useful for clearing validity questions
- Important for follow-on products/generics

#### Strategic Timing:
- Before launching competing product
- Upon receiving infringement notice
- As counterclaim immediately upon suit filing

---

## Section 66: Government Power to Revoke

#### Grounds:
Patent invention used against public interest, specifically:
- Contrary to public order or morality
- Prejudicial to security of India
- Unreasonably restricts trade
- Not working to public benefit

#### Procedure:
1. Central Government forms opinion
2. Notification to patentee with grounds
3. Opportunity to be heard
4. After hearing, revocation ordered if satisfied

- [ ] Appropriate High Court identified
- [ ] Standing as "person interested" established
- [ ] Grounds under Section 64(1) clearly specified
- [ ] Prior art properly cited with priority dates
- [ ] Claims specifically addressed
- [ ] Evidence organized and verified
- [ ] Expert testimony arranged (if needed)
- [ ] Legal representation secured

1. **Wrong forum**: File before appropriate High Court
2. **Insufficient specificity**: Generic grounds fail
3. **Missing evidence**: Claims without proof dismissed
4. **Section 8 pleading**: Must show materiality
5. **Standing issues**: Establish "person interested"

# Protocol: Compulsory Licensing

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-009
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Licensing & Access
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for compulsory licensing under Sections 84, 92, and 92A of the Patents Act, 1970, including application requirements, grounds, and government use provisions under Sections 100-102.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Need to work patented invention without voluntary license
- Patent not worked in India adequately
- Public health emergency requiring access to patented medicines
- Government procurement of patented technology
- Export of pharmaceuticals to countries without manufacturing capacity

---

## Prerequisites

- Three years elapsed from date of patent grant (for Section 84)
- Reasonable efforts to obtain voluntary license (documented)
- Ability to work invention to public advantage
- Understanding of applicable legal provisions

---

## Core Process

### Section 84: Standard Compulsory License

#### Step 1: Verify Eligibility

**Timing Requirement**:
- At least **3 years** from date of patent grant

**Applicant Must Demonstrate**:
- Efforts to obtain voluntary license on reasonable terms (Section 84(6)(iv))
- Ability to work the invention
- Capability to satisfy public interest

#### Step 2: Identify Grounds (Section 84(1))

Three statutory grounds:

| Ground | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| (a) | Reasonable requirements of public not satisfied |
| (b) | Patented invention not available at reasonably affordable price |
| (c) | Patented invention not worked in territory of India |

**Note**: Must establish **at least one** ground.

#### Step 3: Define "Reasonable Requirements" (Section 84(7))

Requirements not satisfied if:
- Demand not adequately met by patent working
- Working prevented by import refusal
- Domestic industry/export prejudiced by import conditions
- Establishment/development of commercial activities prejudiced
- Patent not worked on commercial scale without satisfactory reason

#### Step 4: Prepare Application

**Required Elements**:
1. Nature of applicant's interest
2. Facts establishing case for license
3. Evidence of efforts to obtain voluntary license
4. Particulars of license terms sought
5. Evidence of capability to work invention

**Filing**: Before Controller of Patents

#### Step 5: Controller's Examination

**Factors Considered (Section 84(6))**:
1. Nature of invention
2. Time elapsed since patent grant
3. Applicant's efforts for voluntary license
4. Applicant's ability to work for public advantage
5. Whether conditions include best mode disclosure
6. Whether applicant made efforts to obtain license on reasonable terms

#### Step 6: Terms of Compulsory License

If granted, license typically includes:
- Royalty to patentee (determined by Controller)
- Non-exclusive (usually)
- Predominantly domestic market supply
- Duration limits
- Working requirements

---

### Section 92: National Emergency/Extreme Urgency

#### When Applicable:
- National emergency declared
- Extreme urgency circumstances
- Public non-commercial use

#### Key Differences from Section 84:
- **No 3-year waiting period**
- **No prior negotiation required** (for urgency/emergency)
- Government notification triggers process
- Expedited procedure

#### COVID-19 Context:
Supreme Court in April 2021 noted Central Government could consider Sections 92, 100, 102 for pandemic response.

---

### Section 92A: Export to Countries Without Manufacturing

#### Purpose:
Enable export of pharmaceutical products to countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity.

#### Requirements:
- Importing country has granted CL or permitted import
- Notification received from importing country
- Product only for that country's market

---

### Sections 100-102: Government Use

#### Section 100 - Use for Government Purposes:

**No license needed** - Government may:
- Use any patented invention for government purposes
- Authorize any person to use for government purposes
- During national emergency or extreme urgency, no prior notice required

**Patentee Entitlement**:
- Adequate compensation/royalty
- Terms settled by agreement or High Court

#### Section 102 - Government Acquisition:

**Full Acquisition Power**:
- Central Government may acquire entire patent
- For public purpose
- Rights vest in Government
- Compensation determined by agreement or High Court

---

## Landmark Case: Natco v. Bayer (2012)

**First (and Only) Successful CL in India**:
- Drug: Sorafenib Tosylate (Nexavar) for cancer
- Bayer price: ₹2,80,000/month
- Natco proposed: ₹8,800/month
- Royalty: 6% to Bayer
- Affirmed through IPAB, Bombay HC, and Supreme Court

**Key Learnings**:
1. All three Section 84(1) grounds can be established simultaneously
2. Import ≠ Working in India
3. Price affordability assessed against Indian purchasing power
4. Royalty rate: balances patentee compensation and public access

---

## Failed CL Applications

### BDR v. Bristol-Myers Squibb (2013):
- Drug: Sprycel
- **Rejected**: No genuine effort to obtain voluntary license
- No demonstrated capability to work

### Lee Pharma v. AstraZeneca (2015):
- Drug: Saxagliptin
- **Rejected**: Substitutes available in market
- Reasonable requirements of public satisfied

# Protocol: Prior Art Search Strategy

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-010
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Patentability Analysis
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for conducting prior art searches for Indian patent applications, including database selection, search strategies, and documentation requirements for patentability, freedom-to-operate, and opposition/invalidity purposes.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Evaluating patentability of new invention
- Preparing patent application
- Freedom-to-operate analysis
- Opposition/revocation evidence gathering
- Due diligence in IP transactions

---

## Prerequisites

- Invention disclosure or technical description
- Understanding of relevant technology field
- Access to patent and non-patent databases
- Knowledge of IPC/CPC classification

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Define Search Scope

#### Identify Key Elements:
1. **Technical features** - What the invention does
2. **Problem solved** - Technical challenge addressed
3. **Field of invention** - Technology domain
4. **Closest prior art** - Known existing solutions

#### Determine Search Purpose:

| Purpose | Scope | Date Range |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Patentability | Similar inventions | No time limit |
| FTO | In-force patents | Current + unexpired |
| Opposition | Anticipating prior art | Before priority date |
| Invalidity | All prior art | Before priority date |

### Step 2: Select Databases

#### Indian Patent Database:

**InPASS (Indian Patent Advanced Search System)**
- URL: https://iprsearch.ipindia.gov.in/publicsearch
- Coverage: All Indian patents and applications
- Features: Full-text search, Boolean operators, wildcards
- Best for: Indian prior art, Form 3 compliance check

**Limitations**:
- No bulk download
- Limited Boolean string length
- AND/OR fixed between entries

#### Global Patent Databases:

| Database | Coverage | Best For |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Espacenet | 130+ countries | European/global search |
| PATENTSCOPE | PCT applications | International filings |
| USPTO | US patents | American prior art |
| Google Patents | Multiple countries | Quick keyword search |
| The Lens | Global + scholarly | Comprehensive search |

#### Non-Patent Literature:

- Google Scholar
- PubMed (life sciences)
- IEEE Xplore (electronics/computing)
- ScienceDirect
- arXiv (physics, CS, math)
- ResearchGate
- University theses databases

### Step 3: Develop Search Strategy

#### Keyword Search:

1. **Primary Keywords**: Core technical terms
2. **Synonyms**: Alternative terminology
3. **Related Terms**: Adjacent concepts
4. **Translations**: For foreign databases

**Boolean Operators**:
- AND: Narrow results (term1 AND term2)
- OR: Broaden results (synonym1 OR synonym2)
- NOT: Exclude (term AND NOT exception)

**Wildcards**:
- `*` : Multiple characters (comput* = computer, computing, computational)
- `?` : Single character (defen?e = defense, defence)

#### Classification Search:

**IPC (International Patent Classification)**:
- 8 sections (A-H)
- Hierarchical structure
- Example: A61K 31/00 (Medicinal preparations)

**CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification)**:
- More detailed than IPC
- Used by EPO and USPTO

**Strategy**: Combine classification with keywords for targeted results.

### Step 4: Execute Search

#### Systematic Approach:

1. **Broad search first**: Identify relevant patent families
2. **Refine with classification**: Narrow to specific field
3. **Keyword variations**: Cover synonyms
4. **Date filters**: Apply as appropriate
5. **Assignee/applicant search**: Known competitors

#### InPASS Search Tips:

```
Field Options:
- Title
- Abstract
- Claims
- Full Specification
- Applicant Name
- Inventor Name
- Application Number
- IPC

Search Strategy:
1. Start with title/abstract keywords
2. Add IPC classification
3. Use wildcards for variations
4. Check applicant name for competitors
```

### Step 5: Analyze Results

#### For Each Relevant Document:

1. **Technical Disclosure**: What is taught?
2. **Priority Date**: Effective date of disclosure
3. **Claim Scope**: What is protected?
4. **Status**: Active, expired, pending?
5. **Relevance**: Impact on patentability/FTO

#### Comparison Chart:

```
| Feature | Invention | Prior Art 1 | Prior Art 2 | Prior Art 3 |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Element A | X | X | X | - |
| Element B | X | X | - | X |
| Element C | X | - | X | - |
| Element D | X | - | - | - |
```

### Step 6: Document Search

#### Search Report Should Include:

1. **Search strategy**: Keywords, classifications, databases
2. **Date of search**: Important for ongoing monitoring
3. **Documents found**: Citation details
4. **Relevance assessment**: Impact analysis
5. **Claim mapping**: Features vs. prior art
6. **Conclusion**: Patentability/FTO opinion

---

## Search Types by Purpose

### Patentability Search

**Objective**: Determine if invention is novel and non-obvious

**Scope**:
- All published prior art worldwide
- Patents and non-patent literature
- No date limitation for search

**Key Questions**:
- Is every claim element disclosed in single reference? (anticipation)
- Would combination be obvious to POSITA? (inventive step)

### Freedom-to-Operate Search

**Objective**: Identify in-force patents that may cover proposed activity

**Scope**:
- Focus on granted patents
- Current + unexpired
- India-specific for Indian operations

**Key Questions**:
- Do any claims cover proposed product/process?
- Can design-around be implemented?

### Opposition/Invalidity Search

**Objective**: Find prior art to challenge patent validity

**Scope**:
- Prior art dated before priority date
- Any public disclosure counts
- Including abandoned/expired applications

**Key Focus**:
- Section 8 compliance (foreign filings)
- Section 3 subject matter exclusions
- Best mode non-disclosure

# Protocol: Patentability Analysis

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-011
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Patentability Assessment
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive framework for evaluating patentability of inventions under Indian patent law, including novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability, and subject matter eligibility analysis under Section 3.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Evaluating invention disclosure for patent filing
- Preparing patentability opinion
- Responding to examination objections
- Advising on R&D investment decisions
- Due diligence in technology acquisition

---

## Prerequisites

- Complete invention disclosure
- Prior art search results
- Understanding of technology field
- Knowledge of Section 3 exclusions

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Subject Matter Eligibility (Section 3)

**First hurdle**: Is the invention excluded from patentability?

#### Section 3 Exclusions:

| Section | Exclusion | Examples |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| 3(a) | Frivolous or contrary to law | Perpetual motion machines |
| 3(b) | Contrary to public order/morality | Human cloning |
| 3(c) | Mere discovery of natural substances | Naturally occurring gene sequences |
| 3(d) | New forms without enhanced efficacy | Polymorphs, salts, esters |
| 3(e) | Mere admixture | Simple physical mixtures |
| 3(f) | Mere arrangement/rearrangement | Reconfiguration without technical effect |
| 3(h) | Method of agriculture/horticulture | Farming methods |
| 3(i) | Medical treatment methods | Surgical, therapeutic, diagnostic methods on humans |
| 3(j) | Plants, animals (except microorganisms) | Seeds, varieties, biological processes |
| 3(k) | Mathematical methods, computer programs per se, algorithms | Pure software, business methods |
| 3(l) | Literary, artistic works | Copyright subject matter |
| 3(m) | Mental acts, rules, methods | Game rules |
| 3(n) | Presentation of information | Data display methods |
| 3(o) | Topography of ICs | Covered by separate Act |
| 3(p) | Traditional knowledge | TKDL documented knowledge |

### Step 2: Deep Dive - Critical Section 3 Analyses

#### Section 3(d) - Pharmaceutical Evergreening:

**What's Excluded**:
- New forms of known substances
- Derivatives with same activity
- **UNLESS** enhanced efficacy shown

**Novartis Test (Supreme Court 2013)**:
- "Efficacy" = "therapeutic efficacy" for pharmaceuticals
- Must show **clinical improvement**, not just physicochemical
- Bioavailability alone insufficient
- Comparative data required

**Best Practice**:
- Include comparative efficacy data in specification
- Document clinical improvements
- Avoid claiming mere polymorphs/salts without efficacy data

#### Section 3(k) - Computer-Related Inventions:

**What's Excluded**:
- Mathematical methods
- Business methods
- Computer programs "per se"
- Algorithms

**2025 CRI Guidelines - Technical Effect Test**:

1. Is there a **technical problem** being solved?
2. Does solution involve **technical means**?
3. Is there a **technical effect** beyond normal software execution?

**Allowable CRIs**:
- Technical contribution to computer system operation
- Hardware efficiency improvements
- Network performance optimization
- Security system enhancements

**Not Allowable**:
- Pure business processes implemented in software
- Data manipulation without technical effect
- User interface improvements alone

### Step 3: Novelty Analysis (Section 2(1)(l))

**Test**: Is the invention anticipated by prior art?

#### Prior Art Definition:
- Published anywhere in the world before priority date
- Publicly known or used in India before priority date

#### Anticipation Requirements:
All essential features of claim must be **explicitly or inherently disclosed** in a single prior art reference.

#### Analysis Framework:

```
For each independent claim:
1. List all claim elements
2. Compare element-by-element with closest prior art
3. Identify:
   - Disclosed elements
   - Inherently present elements
   - Missing elements (differentiators)
```

**If any element missing** → Claim is novel

### Step 4: Inventive Step Analysis (Section 2(1)(ja))

**Definition**: "Feature of an invention that involves technical advance as compared to existing knowledge or having economic significance or both and that makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art"

#### TSM (Teaching-Suggestion-Motivation) Test:

Would POSITA (Person of Ordinary Skill in the Art):
1. Have **motivation** to combine references?
2. Have **expectation of success**?
3. Have **teaching** how to combine?

#### Analysis Steps:

1. **Define POSITA**: Educational background, experience level
2. **Identify closest prior art**: Most relevant single reference
3. **Identify differences**: Between claim and prior art
4. **Evaluate obviousness**: Would combination be obvious?

#### Secondary Considerations (may support non-obviousness):
- Commercial success
- Long-felt need
- Failure of others
- Unexpected results
- Industry skepticism

### Step 5: Industrial Applicability (Section 2(1)(ac))

**Test**: Can invention be made or used in any industry?

**Generally satisfied if**:
- Concrete, real-world application identified
- Not mere abstract concept
- Practical utility demonstrated

### Step 6: Sufficiency of Disclosure (Section 10)

**Requirements**:
1. **Full description**: Enable POSITA to practice
2. **Best mode**: Disclose preferred embodiment
3. **Clear claims**: Definite scope of protection
4. **Fair basis**: Claims supported by description

# Protocol: Patent Infringement Litigation

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-012
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Litigation

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for patent infringement litigation in India, covering jurisdiction, remedies, claim construction, infringement analysis, defenses, and recent trends in damages awards.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Patent infringement detected
- Received cease and desist letter
- Preparing infringement suit
- Defending against infringement claim
- Seeking damages for past infringement

---

## Prerequisites

- Valid, in-force Indian patent
- Clear ownership/assignment chain
- Identified infringer and infringing product/process
- Claim construction analysis
- Evidence of infringement

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Pre-Litigation Assessment

#### Patent Validity Check:
- Maintenance fees current?
- Within 20-year term?
- No pending revocation/opposition?

#### Infringement Analysis:
- Claim construction completed?
- Literal infringement or doctrine of equivalents?
- Evidence available?

#### Commercial Assessment:
- Commercial importance of infringing activity
- Defendant's financial position
- Settlement possibility

### Step 2: Jurisdiction Selection

#### Post-IPAB Abolition (2021):
Primary infringement jurisdiction: **High Courts**

#### Territorial Jurisdiction:
- Where plaintiff resides/carries on business
- Where cause of action arises (manufacture/sale)
- **Delhi High Court**: Most active, IP Division established

#### Why Delhi High Court Preferred:
- Specialized IP Division
- Experienced judges
- Recent landmark judgments
- Faster disposal

### Step 3: File Suit for Infringement

#### Required Elements:
1. **Plaint**: Statement of claim
2. **Particulars of Patent**: Complete specification, grant certificate
3. **Infringement Analysis**: Claim chart comparing patent claims vs. infringing product
4. **Evidence**: Purchase samples, catalogues, websites, advertisements

#### Forms of Relief (Section 108):

| Relief | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Injunction | Permanent restraint on infringement |
| Damages | Compensatory damages for loss |
| Accounts of Profit | Disgorgement of infringer's profits |
| Delivery-up | Surrender of infringing goods |
| Costs | Litigation costs |

### Step 4: Interim Relief (Injunctions)

#### Test for Interim Injunction:
1. **Prima facie case**: Valid patent likely infringed
2. **Balance of convenience**: Favors plaintiff
3. **Irreparable injury**: Not compensable by damages

#### Key Considerations:
- Registration alone doesn't entitle to injunction
- Courts lean against monopolies
- Credible validity challenge may deny injunction

#### Pharma Specific (Vulnerability Assessment):
Recent trends: Courts assess patent "vulnerability" to validity challenges before granting interim relief.

### Step 5: Claim Construction

#### Indian Approach:
- Purposive construction (not purely literal)
- Specification as whole considered
- Description informs claim scope
- Prosecution history may be considered

#### Infringement Types:

**Literal Infringement**:
- Every claim element present in accused product
- Element-by-element comparison

**Doctrine of Equivalents**:
- Equivalent elements performing substantially same function
- In substantially same way
- Achieving substantially same result

### Step 6: Trial Proceedings

#### Typical Timeline:
- **2-4 years** for final judgment
- Expedited in IP Division courts

#### Procedural Steps:
1. Plaint filing
2. Written statement by defendant
3. Framing of issues
4. Discovery and evidence
5. Arguments
6. Judgment

### Step 7: Damages Assessment

#### 2024 Landmark Awards:

| Case | Damages | Year |
|------|---------|------|
| Ericsson v. Lava | ₹244 Crore (~$29M) | 2024 |
| CCA v. Mobi Antenna | ₹216 Crore (~$26M) | 2024 |

#### Calculation Methods:

**Lost Profits**:
- Profits plaintiff would have made
- But for infringement

**Reasonable Royalty**:
- Hypothetical negotiated royalty
- Based on comparable licenses

**SEP/FRAND Royalties**:
- For Standard Essential Patents
- Comparable licensing approach preferred

#### Pre-Grant Damages (Section 11A(7)):
- Available from publication date
- Cannot institute suit until grant

### Step 8: Defenses to Infringement

#### Section 107 Defenses:

| Defense | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Non-infringement | Accused product doesn't fall within claims |
| Invalidity | Patent invalid on Section 64 grounds |
| Prior use | Defendant had prior user rights |
| License | Defendant is licensee |
| Government use | Use under Section 47 |
| Experimental use | Research/educational purpose |
| Parallel import | Exhaustion defense |

#### Invalidity as Defense:
- Raises same grounds as Section 64
- Creates in personam effect only
- Requires Section 71(1) for register correction

# Protocol: Section 8 Foreign Filing Compliance

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-014
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Compliance
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for compliance with Section 8 of the Patents Act, 1970, covering disclosure obligations for corresponding foreign applications, Form 3 requirements, and the significant relaxations under the 2024 Amendment Rules.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Filing patent application in India
- Filing corresponding application abroad
- Receiving First Examination Report (FER)
- Reviewing prosecution history before grant
- Evaluating opposition/revocation risk

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of patent family
- Tracking system for foreign filings
- Knowledge of prosecution status abroad
- Awareness of deadlines

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Section 8 Requirements

#### Section 8(1) - Information Disclosure:
Applicant must disclose:
- Applications filed in any country outside India
- For same or substantially same invention

**Form 3 Filing Timeline (Pre-2024)**:
- At filing, or within 6 months
- Updated whenever status changes

#### Section 8(2) - Controller's Direction:
Controller may require:
- Information about prosecution in other countries
- Search reports from other offices
- Office actions and responses

### Step 2: 2024 Amendment Changes (Critical Update)

#### Major Relaxation - New Rule:

**Before 2024**:
- Continuous Form 3 updates required
- Every change in foreign applications
- Heavy compliance burden

**After March 15, 2024**:
- **Only TWO Form 3 submissions required**:

| Submission | Timing | Content |
|------------|--------|---------|
| First Form 3 | At filing OR within 6 months | All foreign applications filed |
| Updated Form 3 | Within 3 months of FER | Updated status of all applications |

#### What This Means:
- No more continuous filings
- Simplified compliance
- One update after FER captures prosecution status

### Step 3: Form 3 Content Requirements

#### Information to Include:

1. **Country/Office**: Where application filed
2. **Application Number**: Official number assigned
3. **Filing Date**: Date of filing
4. **Priority Claimed**: If priority claimed from another application
5. **Current Status**: Pending/Granted/Refused/Withdrawn/Abandoned
6. **Grant Details**: If granted - patent number, grant date

#### Template Declaration:

```

```

### Step 4: Prosecution History Compliance (Section 8(2))

#### When Controller May Direct:

During examination, Controller may request:
- Search reports from USPTO, EPO, JPO, etc.
- Office actions (First, Final)
- Applicant's responses/amendments
- Claims as amended/granted abroad

#### Response Deadline:
As directed by Controller (typically within examination response period)

#### Best Practice:
Proactively file search reports from major offices with FER response

### Step 5: Consequences of Non-Compliance

#### Severe Penalties:

| Consequence | Ground |
|-------------|--------|
| Pre-grant opposition | Section 25(1)(h) |
| Post-grant opposition | Section 25(2)(h) |
| Revocation | Section 64(1)(m) |

#### Case Law Trend:
Non-compliance increasingly scrutinized. Courts have revoked patents for material non-disclosures.

#### What Constitutes Non-Compliance:
- Complete failure to file Form 3
- Failure to update after FER
- False/misleading information
- Withholding material prosecution results
- Not disclosing rejections abroad

### Step 6: Materiality Standard

#### Key Question:
Would the undisclosed information have affected examination?

#### Material Information:
- Prior art cited in foreign office action
- Rejections for novelty/inventive step
- Claim amendments narrowing scope
- Examiner interviews affecting prosecution

#### Potentially Not Material:

# Protocol: Traditional Knowledge and TKDL

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-015
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Subject Matter Exclusions
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide on traditional knowledge protection under Indian patent law, including Section 3(p) exclusion, Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL), biopiracy prevention, and access and benefit sharing requirements.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Evaluating invention based on traditional practices
- Conducting prior art search in traditional medicine
- Filing opposition based on traditional knowledge
- Assessing biopiracy risk in invention
- Access and benefit sharing compliance

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of traditional knowledge concepts
- Access to TKDL (for patent offices)
- Knowledge of Biological Diversity Act, 2002
- Familiarity with Section 3 exclusions

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Section 3(p) Exclusion

#### Section 3(p) - Traditional Knowledge:
"An invention which, in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components"

#### What's Excluded:
- Direct use of traditional knowledge
- Aggregation of known traditional properties
- Duplication of traditional formulations

#### What May Be Patentable:
- Novel derivatives with enhanced efficacy (subject to 3(d))
- New combinations with non-obvious synergy
- New applications genuinely not known traditionally

### Step 2: Understanding TKDL

#### Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL):

**Established**: 2001 (CSIR + Ministry of AYUSH)

**Purpose**: Prevent biopiracy by documenting traditional knowledge as searchable prior art

**Coverage** (as of 2024):
| System | Formulations |
|--------|--------------|
| Ayurveda | 127,533 |
| Unani | 240,850 |
| Siddha | 70,158 |
| Sowa Rigpa | 5,445 |
| Yoga | 4,778 |
| **Total** | **~448,764** |

#### Languages:
- Original: Sanskrit, Hindi, Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Tamil, Bhoti
- Transcribed into: English, French, German, Spanish, Japanese

### Step 3: TKDL Access Framework

#### Access Agreements:
16 international patent offices have access:
- Indian Patent Office (IPO)
- European Patent Office (EPO)
- United States Patent Office (USPTO)
- Japan Patent Office (JPO)
- German Patent Office
- Canadian Patent Office
- UK Patent Office
- Australian Patent Office
- And 8 others

#### How Patent Offices Use TKDL:
1. Search TKDL during examination
2. Cite as prior art if relevant
3. Reject/oppose applications based on TKDL evidence

#### Impact:
**283+ patent applications** refused, amended, or withdrawn based on TKDL evidence (as of 2024)

### Step 4: Conducting TKDL-Aware Prior Art Search

#### If You're an Applicant:

**Before Filing**:
1. Search publicly available traditional medicine literature
2. Review Ayurvedic/Unani/Siddha pharmacopoeias
3. Check academic literature on traditional uses
4. Consider if claimed invention merely documents traditional practice

**Strategy**:
- Identify what is genuinely novel beyond traditional knowledge
- Document inventive contribution clearly
- Prepare to distinguish from traditional practices

#### If You're Opposing/Challenging:

**Sources Beyond TKDL** (for non-patent office access):
- Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India
- Unani Pharmacopoeia of India
- Siddha Pharmacopoeia of India
- Indian Materia Medica
- Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita (classical texts)
- Published academic research on traditional medicine
- People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs)

### Step 5: Biological Diversity Act Compliance

#### Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS):

**Biological Diversity Act, 2002** requires:

| Applicant Type | Requirement |
|----------------|-------------|
| Foreign entity | Prior approval from National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) |
| Indian company with foreign participation | Prior approval from NBA |
| Indian citizen/entity | Prior intimation to State Biodiversity Board |

#### For Patent Applications:
- Section 6: Prior approval before applying for IP rights on biological resources
- Failure to comply: Patent opposition/revocation ground

#### National Biodiversity Authority (NBA):
- Reviews applications for access to biological resources
- Ensures benefit sharing with local communities
- Can impose conditions on IP rights

### Step 6: Famous Biopiracy Cases

#### Turmeric Patent (1995 - USPTO):
- US Patent 5,401,504 on wound healing use
- CSIR challenged based on traditional knowledge
- **Revoked** by USPTO in 1997

#### Neem Patent (1995 - EPO):
- EP Patent 0436257 on neem-based fungicide
- Challenged by Indian NGOs
- **Revoked** by EPO in 2005

#### Basmati Patent (1997):
- RiceTec's US patent on "Basmati" rice
- India challenged as geographic indication/TK
- Claims narrowed significantly

---

## Patentability Assessment for TK-Related Inventions

### Decision Tree:

```
Is the invention:
│
├─ Direct traditional practice → NOT PATENTABLE (3(p))
│
├─ Aggregation of known TK properties → NOT PATENTABLE (3(p))
│
├─ Duplication of TK formulation → NOT PATENTABLE (3(p))
│
├─ New form of TK substance → Subject to 3(d) analysis
│   ├─ Enhanced efficacy shown → May be patentable
│   └─ No enhanced efficacy → NOT PATENTABLE
│
├─ Novel combination with synergy → Analyze novelty + inventive step
│   └─ Non-obvious synergistic effect → May be patentable
│
└─ Genuinely new application → Analyze as normal invention
    └─ Novel + Non-obvious + No TK prior art → May be patentable
```

## Opposition/Invalidity Based on Traditional Knowledge

### Section 25(1)(k) & Section 64(1)(q):

**Ground**: Subject matter which "is traditional knowledge or...aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component"

### Evidence Sources:
1. Classical texts (with translations)
2. Pharmacopoeias
3. TKDL citations (via patent office)
4. Academic literature
5. Expert declarations from Ayurveda/traditional medicine practitioners

# Protocol: Computer-Related Inventions (CRI)

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-017
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Subject Matter Eligibility
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for patenting computer-related inventions in India under Section 3(k), incorporating the 2025 CRI Guidelines, technical effect analysis, and strategies for software, AI/ML, and blockchain inventions.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Filing software/AI/ML patent application
- Responding to Section 3(k) objection
- Evaluating patentability of computer-implemented invention
- Drafting claims for CRI application
- Analyzing CRI prior art landscape

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of Section 3(k) exclusions
- Knowledge of 2025 CRI Guidelines
- Technical description of invention
- Awareness of technical effect requirement

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Section 3(k) Framework

#### Section 3(k) Exclusions:
"A mathematical or business method or a computer program per se or algorithms"

#### Key Terms:
- **Mathematical method**: Abstract mathematical operations
- **Business method**: Commercial/financial practices
- **Computer program per se**: Pure software without technical application
- **Algorithm**: Abstract computational procedure

#### Critical Distinction:
**"Per se"** = Computer programs *alone*, without technical application

### Step 2: 2025 CRI Guidelines Framework

#### Release History:
- Draft v1.0: March 2025
- Draft v2.0: June 2025
- Final Guidelines: July 29, 2025

#### Key Principles:

1. **Substance over Form**: Examine invention as a whole, not just claim format
2. **Technical Effect Test**: Central to patentability determination
3. **Hardware Integration**: Consider technical means used
4. **40+ Scenario Examples**: Practical guidance provided

### Step 3: Three-Step Technical Effect Test

#### Step 1: Identify Technical Problem
- What technical challenge does the invention address?
- Is it a genuine technical problem (not business/administrative)?

#### Step 2: Assess Technical Means
- Does solution employ technical means?
- Hardware components, system architecture, data processing?

#### Step 3: Evaluate Technical Effect
- Does invention produce technical effect?
- Effect on computer operation, network, hardware?

### Step 4: What Constitutes "Technical Effect"

#### Allowable Technical Effects:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **System Performance** | Reduced latency, memory optimization, faster processing |
| **Resource Efficiency** | Lower bandwidth usage, reduced power consumption |
| **Security Enhancement** | Encryption, authentication, access control |
| **Hardware Control** | Device operation, sensor integration, machine control |
| **Network Improvement** | Packet handling, load balancing, routing optimization |
| **Data Integrity** | Error correction, data validation, compression |

#### NOT Technical Effects:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Business efficiency | Cost reduction, revenue optimization |
| User convenience | Easier interface, fewer clicks |
| Data presentation | New display format, visualization alone |
| Mental processes | Decision support without technical implementation |
| Commercial outcomes | Market analysis, pricing strategies |

### Step 5: Claim Drafting Strategies

#### System/Apparatus Claims (Preferred):

```
A system for [technical function] comprising:
- a processor configured to execute...;
- a memory storing instructions that, when executed, cause the processor to...;
- a [hardware component] for [technical function];
wherein the system achieves [technical effect].
```

#### Method Claims (With Technical Steps):

```
A computer-implemented method for [technical result], comprising:
a) receiving, by a processor, input data from [technical source];
b) processing the input data using [specific algorithm/technique];
c) generating output data that [technical effect];
d) transmitting the output data to [technical destination/device].
```

#### Avoid:
- "A method of conducting business..."
- "A computer program for..."
- "An algorithm for calculating..."
- Pure data manipulation without technical context

### Step 6: AI/ML Specific Guidance

#### 2025 Guidelines on AI/ML:

**Patentable (with technical effect)**:
- ML model improving system performance
- AI-driven hardware control
- Neural network architecture with specific technical advantage
- Training techniques with measurable system benefit

**Likely Non-Patentable**:
- Generic ML model application
- Business prediction algorithms
- Data analytics for business decisions
- AI-generated content/creations

#### Required Disclosures for AI/ML:
- Network architecture details
- Parameter settings
- Training methodology
- Technical performance metrics

### Step 7: Blockchain/DLT Inventions

#### Potentially Patentable:
- Consensus mechanism improvements
- Cryptographic enhancements
- Network security protocols
- Data integrity verification systems

#### Likely Non-Patentable:
- Smart contracts implementing business logic
- Token economics/financial instruments
- Governance models
- Pure record-keeping applications

### Step 8: Responding to Section 3(k) Objections

#### Strategy Framework:

1. **Identify Technical Problem**: Clearly state technical challenge
2. **Highlight Technical Means**: Point to hardware/system elements
3. **Demonstrate Technical Effect**: Quantify improvement if possible
4. **Cite Case Law**: Reference favorable decisions
5. **Distinguish "Per Se"**: Show not mere program but technical implementation

#### Key Case Law to Cite:

| Case | Holding |
|------|---------|
| **Ferid Allani v. UoI (2020)** | Technical effect analysis required |
| **Microsoft v. Asst. Controller (2023)** | Effect on hardware/system efficacy patentable |
| **OpenTV v. Controller (2024)** | Technical contribution standard |

---

## Example Analysis

### Example 1: Patentable CRI

**Invention**: "A system for reducing network latency in video streaming"

**Technical Problem**: Network congestion causing video buffering

**Technical Means**: Processor analyzing packet flow, predictive caching algorithm implemented in router

**Technical Effect**: 40% reduction in latency, improved bandwidth utilization

**Claim**: "A video streaming system comprising a processor configured to analyze network packet flow and implement predictive caching, wherein network latency is reduced by at least 30%..."

**Analysis**: ✅ Technical problem, technical means, measurable technical effect → Likely patentable

### Example 2: Non-Patentable (Business Method)

**Invention**: "A method for dynamic pricing based on demand prediction"

**Technical Problem**: Revenue optimization (business, not technical)

**Technical Means**: Standard computer processing

**Technical Effect**: None beyond normal program execution

**Claim**: "A method for adjusting product prices comprising analyzing demand patterns and modifying price displays..."

**Analysis**: ❌ Business problem, no technical effect → Section 3(k) excluded

# Protocol: Standard Essential Patents (SEPs)

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-018
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Licensing & Standards
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for standard essential patents in India, covering FRAND licensing obligations, SEP litigation, royalty determination, and recent landmark judgments including Ericsson v. Lava (2024).

---

## Activation Triggers

- Implementing technology standard (4G/5G, WiFi, etc.)
- Receiving SEP licensing demand
- Filing SEP infringement suit
- Negotiating FRAND license
- Determining reasonable royalty rates

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of relevant technology standard
- Knowledge of patent portfolio
- FRAND commitment awareness
- Comparable license data (if available)

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understanding SEPs in India

#### Definition:
A **Standard Essential Patent (SEP)** is a patent claiming technology essential to implementing a technical standard (e.g., 3G, 4G LTE, 5G, WiFi, HEVC).

#### Legal Framework:
- No specific SEP legislation in India
- Patents Act, 1970 applies generally
- FRAND commitments enforced through contract/equity
- Competition Commission of India (CCI) jurisdiction

#### Standard Setting Organizations (SSOs) in India:
- Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)
- Telecom Engineering Centre (TEC)
- TSDSI (Telecommunications Standards Development Society, India)

### Step 2: FRAND Obligations

#### FRAND = Fair, Reasonable, and Non-Discriminatory

#### What FRAND Requires:

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Fair** | Not exploitative, considering value of patent |
| **Reasonable** | Commercially reasonable royalty rate |
| **Non-Discriminatory** | Similar terms to similarly situated licensees |

#### FRAND Commitment:
- Made to SSO when patent declared essential
- Creates obligation to license to willing licensees
- Not a waiver of patent rights
- Courts enforce through equitable remedies

### Step 3: Willing Licensee Framework

#### Key Principle:
SEP holder must offer FRAND license to **willing licensees**

#### Characteristics of Willing Licensee:
1. Engages in good faith negotiations
2. Doesn't unreasonably delay
3. Provides necessary information
4. Accepts reasonable FRAND terms
5. Doesn't engage in reverse hold-up

#### Characteristics of Unwilling Licensee:
1. Delays negotiations unreasonably
2. Refuses reasonable offers
3. Uses litigation as delay tactic
4. Doesn't engage in good faith

### Step 4: FRAND Royalty Determination

#### Methodologies Recognized in India:

**1. Comparable Licenses Approach** (Preferred in India)
- Look to existing FRAND licenses
- Similarly situated licensees
- Same technology/standard
- *Endorsed in Ericsson v. Lava (2024)*

**2. Top-Down Approach**
- Start with total aggregate royalty for standard
- Apportion among all SEPs
- Calculate per-patent royalty

**3. Incremental Value Approach**
- Value added by patented technology
- Over next-best alternative

#### Royalty Base Debate:

| Position | Argument |
|----------|----------|
| **End-Product (SSPPU)** | Entire mobile device price |
| **Component (Chipset)** | Value of specific component |

**India Position (Ericsson v. Lava)**: For mobile devices where telecom connectivity is core functionality, **end-product level** calculation is appropriate.

### Step 5: Key Indian SEP Cases

#### Ericsson v. Lava International (Delhi HC, March 2024):

**Award**: ₹244 Crore (~$29 million)

**Key Holdings**:
- FRAND royalty rate: 1.05% of net selling price
- Comparable licensing approach endorsed
- End-product royalty base for mobile devices
- Willingness to license analyzed

**Methodology**:
- Examined Ericsson's global licensing program
- Considered rates paid by other licensees
- Adjusted for India market conditions

#### Ericsson v. Micromax (2014-2016):
- First major SEP litigation in India
- Interim injunction granted
- Established SEP enforcement precedent

### Step 6: SEP Holder Strategies

#### Pre-Litigation:
1. Declare patents to SSO as essential
2. Maintain FRAND commitment
3. Send licensing offer with specific terms
4. Document negotiation history
5. Be prepared to prove essentiality

#### Litigation:
1. Establish patent validity
2. Prove infringement (claim charts)
3. Demonstrate FRAND compliance
4. Show implementer is unwilling
5. Seek injunction and damages

### Step 7: Implementer Strategies

#### Pre-Litigation:
1. Engage in good faith negotiations
2. Request claim charts showing essentiality
3. Make counter-offers
4. Seek portfolio-wide license
5. Document willingness to license

#### Defense Strategies:
1. Challenge essentiality
2. Challenge validity
3. Argue FRAND breach
4. Challenge royalty calculation
5. CCI competition law complaint

### Step 8: Competition Law Considerations

#### CCI Jurisdiction:
- Section 4 (Abuse of Dominance) may apply
- SEP holder may have dominant position
- Exploitative pricing possible violation
- Refusal to license potential concern

#### CCI Cases:
- Multiple complaints filed against SEP holders
- CCI has investigated FRAND abuse allegations
- No final adverse orders to date

# Protocol: Pharmaceutical Patents & Section 3(d)

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-019
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Pharmaceutical IP
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for pharmaceutical patent strategy in India, focusing on Section 3(d) "enhanced efficacy" requirements, the Novartis precedent, and practical strategies for protecting pharmaceutical innovations.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Filing pharmaceutical patent application
- Responding to Section 3(d) objection
- Evaluating patentability of new drug form
- Assessing incremental pharmaceutical innovation
- Opposition/revocation based on Section 3(d)

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of Section 3(d) framework
- Novartis Supreme Court decision knowledge
- Clinical/pharmaceutical data for efficacy claims
- Prior art landscape analysis

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understanding Section 3(d)

#### Statutory Text:
"The mere discovery of a new form of a known substance which does not result in the enhancement of the known efficacy of that substance or the mere discovery of any new property or new use for a known substance or of the mere use of a known process, machine or apparatus unless such known process results in a new product or employs at least one new reactant"

#### Explanation Clause:
"For the purposes of this clause, salts, esters, ethers, polymorphs, metabolites, pure form, particle size, isomers, mixtures of isomers, complexes, combinations and other derivatives of known substance shall be considered to be the same substance, unless they differ significantly in properties with regard to efficacy"

### Step 2: What Section 3(d) Excludes

| Category | Example | Excluded? |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| New salt of known compound | Imatinib mesylate | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| New polymorph | Beta crystalline form | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| New ester/ether | Valacyclovir (ester of acyclovir) | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| Metabolite | Active metabolite of prodrug | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| Pure form | Racemic → single enantiomer | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| Particle size variation | Nanosized formulation | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| Isomers/stereoisomers | R-isomer vs. racemic | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| New combination | Fixed dose combination | Yes, unless enhanced efficacy |
| New use of known drug | Repurposed drug | Yes |

### Step 3: The Novartis Test (Supreme Court 2013)

#### Case Background:
- **Drug**: Glivec (Imatinib mesylate) for cancer
- **Claim**: Beta crystalline form of imatinib mesylate
- **Argument**: 30% improved bioavailability

#### Supreme Court Holding:

**"Efficacy" for pharmaceuticals = "Therapeutic Efficacy"**

**Not Sufficient**:
- Physicochemical improvements alone
- Bioavailability increase
- Stability improvements
- Better processability

**Required**:
- **Clinical therapeutic benefit** proven
- Better treatment outcome
- Reduced side effects (therapeutic benefit)
- Lower effective dose (proven clinically)

#### Key Quotes:
- "Efficacy means the ability to produce a desired or intended result"
- "For pharmaceuticals, test of efficacy can only be therapeutic efficacy"
- Section 3(d) is "second tier of qualifying standards for chemicals, especially pharmaceutical products"

### Step 4: What Constitutes "Enhanced Efficacy"

#### Potentially Acceptable:

| Improvement | Evidence Required |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Superior clinical outcomes | Comparative clinical trial data |
| Reduced side effects | Clinical safety data |
| Lower effective dose | Dose-response clinical data |
| Extended duration of action | Pharmacodynamic clinical data |
| Better patient compliance (therapeutic impact) | Clinical evidence of outcome improvement |

#### Not Sufficient on Its Own:

| Property | Why Insufficient |
|----------|------------------|
| Higher bioavailability | Not directly therapeutic without clinical correlation |
| Better stability | Manufacturing convenience, not therapy |
| Easier formulation | Manufacturing, not therapeutic |
| Purity improvement | Unless clinically significant |
| Solubility enhancement | Physical property unless clinical benefit proven |

### Step 5: Evidence Strategy

#### Best Practice - Include in Specification:

1. **Comparative Efficacy Data**:
   - Known substance vs. new form
   - Same therapeutic indication
   - Clinical or validated preclinical models

2. **Therapeutic Endpoint Measurements**:
   - Disease biomarkers
   - Clinical outcomes
   - Safety parameters

3. **Statistical Significance**:
   - Proper study design
   - Adequate sample size
   - Meaningful improvement threshold

#### Declaration Strategy:
- Expert declarations explaining therapeutic relevance
- Peer-reviewed publications supporting claims
- Regulatory submission data (if available)

### Step 6: Claim Drafting for Section 3(d) Compliance

#### Include in Claims:

```
"A pharmaceutical composition comprising [new form] of [known compound],
wherein said composition demonstrates enhanced therapeutic efficacy
characterized by [specific therapeutic improvement]"
```

#### Specification Must Support:
- Detailed comparative data
- Statistical analysis
- Clear therapeutic benefit articulation

### Step 7: Common Section 3(d) Objection Responses

#### Strategy Framework:

1. **Challenge "Known Substance" Premise**:
   - Is the parent compound truly "known"?
   - Was it ever used therapeutically?

2. **Demonstrate Enhanced Efficacy**:
   - Provide clinical/preclinical data
   - Quantify therapeutic improvement
   - Distinguish from mere bioavailability

3. **Argue Novel Technical Effect**:
   - Not just new form, but new therapeutic application
   - Unexpected superior activity

4. **Alternative Claim Strategies**:
   - Formulation claims (if novel composition)
   - Process claims (if novel synthesis)
   - Combination claims (if synergistic)

## Section 3(d) Checklist

### Pre-Filing Assessment:
- [ ] Is this a new form of known substance?
- [ ] If yes, what enhanced efficacy data exists?
- [ ] Is therapeutic (not just physical) improvement documented?
- [ ] Comparative data against known substance available?
- [ ] Clinical or validated preclinical evidence?

### Specification Drafting:
- [ ] Efficacy data included in specification
- [ ] Comparative experiments described
- [ ] Statistical analysis provided

| Patent term | No easy extensions through new forms |

### Successful Strategies:
1. **Novel compounds**: NCEs (new chemical entities) not subject to 3(d)
2. **Novel combinations**: If synergistic effect proven
3. **Novel formulations**: If therapeutically superior
4. **Process patents**: For novel manufacturing methods

- Receiving notice for non-filing
- Compulsory licensing defense preparation
- Working status assessment

## Prerequisites

- Granted Indian patent
- Production/import records
- Licensing agreement details (if applicable)

- Understanding of "working" definition

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understanding Working Requirements

#### Legal Framework:
- **Section 83**: General principles on working
- **Section 146**: Patentee to furnish information
- **Rule 131**: Statement regarding working of patent

#### Policy Rationale:
- Patents granted as social contract
- Quid pro quo: monopoly for disclosure AND working
- Public interest in availability of patented products
- Data for compulsory licensing decisions

### Step 2: Form 27 Requirements

#### Who Must File:
- Every patentee (including joint patentees)

#### What to Disclose:

|-------|---------------------|

| Meeting public demand | Adequate quantity at reasonable price |

### Step 3: Filing Procedure

#### Timeline:
- **Filing period**: Within 6 months from end of each calendar year
- **Due date**: June 30 for previous calendar year
- **First filing**: After calendar year of grant

#### How to File:
1. Login to Indian Patent Office e-filing portal
2. Navigate to Form 27 submission
3. Enter patent details
4. Provide working information
5. Upload supporting documents (if any)
6. Pay filing fee
7. Submit and obtain acknowledgment

#### Fees:
| Entity | E-filing Fee |
|--------|--------------|
| Natural Person/Startup | ₹800 |
| Small Entity | ₹2,000 |
| Large Entity | ₹4,000 |

### Step 4: What Constitutes "Working"

#### Definition (Per Section 83):
Working includes:
- Manufacture in India
- Import and sale in India
- Licensing for manufacture in India

#### Adequate Working Factors:
1. **Reasonable requirements**: Is public demand met?
2. **Reasonable price**: Affordable to public?

**Option 3**: Obtain license

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for patent valuation in India, covering valuation methodologies, factors affecting value, regulatory requirements, and practical applications for licensing, transactions, financing, and litigation damages.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Licensing negotiation requiring royalty basis
- M&A transaction with IP assets
- IP collateralization for financing
- Litigation damages calculation
- Technology transfer pricing
- Financial reporting requirements
- Insurance valuation

---

## Prerequisites

- Patent portfolio documentation
- Financial projections
- Market analysis
- Comparable transaction data (if available)
- Understanding of valuation methodologies

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understand Valuation Context

#### Purpose-Specific Valuation:

| Context | Primary Methodology | Key Considerations |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Licensing** | Income approach | Royalty rates, market size |
| **M&A** | Market + Income | Comparable transactions |
| **Financing** | Cost or Market | Liquidation value, marketability |
| **Litigation** | Income approach | Lost profits, reasonable royalty |
| **Tax/Transfer Pricing** | Arm's length price | OECD guidelines, comparables |
| **Financial Reporting** | Fair value (Ind AS) | Impairment testing |

### Step 2: Three Primary Valuation Approaches

#### A. Income Approach

**Principle**: Value based on future economic benefits

**Methods**:
1. **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**
   - Project patent-related cash flows
   - Discount to present value
   - Consider remaining patent life

2. **Relief from Royalty**
   - Estimate royalty savings from ownership
   - Apply market royalty rate
   - Discount to present value

3. **Excess Earnings**
   - Calculate total business earnings
   - Subtract returns on other assets
   - Attribute residual to patent

**Formula (Relief from Royalty)**:
```
Patent Value = Σ [(Revenue × Royalty Rate × (1-Tax)) / (1+r)^t]

Where:
- Revenue = Projected revenue from patented product
- Royalty Rate = Market comparable rate
- Tax = Applicable tax rate
- r = Discount rate
- t = Time period (remaining patent life)
```

#### B. Market Approach

**Principle**: Value based on comparable transactions

**Methods**:
1. **Comparable Transactions**
   - Identify similar patent sales/licenses
   - Adjust for differences
   - Apply multiples

2. **Industry Standards**
   - Standard royalty rates by industry
   - "25% rule" (of gross profit) - now disfavored
   - Industry-specific benchmarks

**Data Sources for India**:
- RoyaltyStat database
- ktMINE
- Public licensing disclosures
- Court damage awards (Ericsson v. Lava)

#### C. Cost Approach

**Principle**: Value based on cost to recreate

**Methods**:
1. **Reproduction Cost**
   - Actual cost to develop patent
   - R&D expenses, prosecution costs

2. **Replacement Cost**
   - Cost to develop equivalent today
   - Adjusted for obsolescence

**Limitations**:
- Does not reflect market value
- Ignores economic benefits
- Best as floor value

### Step 3: Key Value Factors

#### Legal Factors:

| Factor | Impact on Value |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Claim scope** | Broader = Higher value |
| **Remaining term** | Longer = Higher value |
| **Geographic coverage** | More countries = Higher |
| **Validity strength** | Stronger = Higher value |
| **Enforcement history** | Successful = Higher |
| **Prosecution history** | Clean = Higher value |

#### Technical Factors:

| Factor | Impact on Value |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Technology lifecycle** | Mature vs. emerging |
| **Design-around ease** | Harder = Higher value |
| **Standard essentiality** | SEP = Higher value |
| **Technical contribution** | Greater = Higher |
| **Improvement patents** | May reduce value |

#### Commercial Factors:

| Factor | Impact on Value |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Market size** | Larger = Higher value |
| **Market growth** | Higher = Higher value |
| **Competition** | Less = Higher value |
| **Implementation cost** | Lower = Higher value |
| **Revenue potential** | Higher = Higher value |

### Step 4: Indian-Specific Considerations

#### Section 3 Risk Factors:
- **Section 3(d)**: Pharmaceutical new form patents - validity risk
- **Section 3(k)**: Software/CRI patents - enforcement risk
- **Section 3(p)**: Traditional knowledge - biopiracy risk

#### Compulsory Licensing Risk:
- Section 84 availability after 3 years

- Public interest considerations

#### Transfer Pricing (India):
- OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines

#### Foreign Exchange:

### Step 5: Valuation for Litigation Damages

#### Damage Types Under Patents Act:

| Type | Calculation |
|------|-------------|
| **Lost Profits** | Patentee's lost sales × margin |
| **Reasonable Royalty** | Hypothetical negotiation rate |
| **Account of Profits** | Infringer's profits from infringement |

#### Indian Damage Awards (Recent):
- Ericsson v. Lava (2024): ₹244 Crore
- CCA v. Dinesh Gupta (2023): ₹216 Crore
- Bayer v. Cadila (2021): ₹25 Lakh

#### Georgia-Pacific Factors (adapted for India):
1. Established royalty rates
2. Rates paid by licensee for similar patents
3. Nature and scope of license
4. Licensor's policy on licensing
5. Commercial relationship
6. Effect on derivative sales
7. Duration of patent
8. Profitability of products
9. Utility/advantages over alternatives
10. Nature of patented invention
11. Extent of use by infringer
12. Customary profit/selling price
13. Portion of profit attributable to patent
14. Expert testimony

### Step 6: Valuation Report Requirements

#### Standard Components:

1. **Executive Summary**
   - Valuation conclusion
   - Key assumptions
   - Methodology used

2. **Patent Description**
   - Technical description

3. **Market Analysis**
   - Industry overview

   - Rationale for approach

# Protocol: Biotechnology Patents

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-024
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Subject Matter Specific
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for biotechnology patent strategy in India, covering Section 3(j) exclusions, microorganism patents under Budapest Treaty, plant variety protection, biological material deposits, and regulatory considerations.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Filing biotechnology patent application
- Responding to Section 3(j) objection
- Depositing biological material
- Evaluating plant-related invention patentability
- Assessing Biological Diversity Act compliance

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of Section 3(j) framework
- Budapest Treaty deposit knowledge
- Biological Diversity Act awareness
- Technical description of biotechnology invention
- Sequence listings (if applicable)

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understanding Section 3(j) Exclusions

#### Section 3(j) - Plants, Animals, Biological Processes:

"Plants and animals in whole or any part thereof other than micro-organisms but including seeds, varieties and species and essentially biological processes for production or propagation of plants and animals"

#### What Section 3(j) Excludes:

| Category | Excluded? | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Plants (whole) | Yes | Always excluded |
| Plant parts | Yes | Leaves, stems, roots, etc. |
| Seeds | Yes | Explicitly excluded |
| Plant varieties | Yes | PPVFR Act applies |
| Animals (whole) | Yes | Always excluded |
| Animal parts | Yes | Organs, tissues, etc. |
| Essentially biological processes | Yes | Natural reproduction |

#### What Section 3(j) Permits:

| Category | Patentable? | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Microorganisms** | Yes | Explicit carve-out |
| Technical processes | Yes | Non-essentially biological |
| Modified genes/sequences | Possibly | With technical application |
| Biotech products | Possibly | If not plant/animal part |

### Step 2: Microorganism Patents

#### Definition (Indian Jurisprudence):
- Bacteria, fungi, algae, protozoa, viruses
- Plasmids, cell lines (disputed)
- Genetically modified microorganisms

#### Requirements for Patenting:

1. **Novelty**: New microorganism or new use
2. **Inventive step**: Non-obvious modification
3. **Industrial application**: Useful application
4. **Not naturally occurring**: Must be isolated or modified
5. **Enablement**: Reproducible disclosure

#### Budapest Treaty Deposit:

**International Depositary Authorities (IDAs) in India**:
- Microbial Type Culture Collection (MTCC), Chandigarh
- Microbial Culture Collection (MCC), Pune

**Deposit Requirements**:
| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **When** | Before or on filing date |
| **What** | Viable sample of microorganism |
| **Where** | IDA under Budapest Treaty |
| **Reference** | Accession number in specification |

**Deposit Timeline**:
```
Invention → Deposit at IDA → Receive accession number →
File patent application → Include deposit details in specification
```

### Step 3: Sequence Listing Requirements

#### When Required:
- Nucleotide sequences (10+ nucleotides)
- Amino acid sequences (4+ amino acids)

#### Format (Rule 9(3)):
- WIPO Standard ST.25 format
- Separate sequence listing annex
- Computer-readable format
- Proper sequence identifiers

#### Required Information:
```
<110> Applicant Name
<120> Title of Invention
<130> Application Reference
<140> Application Number
<141> Filing Date
<160> Number of Sequences
<210> SEQ ID NO: 1
<211> Length
<212> Type (DNA/RNA/PRT)
<213> Organism
<400> Sequence
```

### Step 4: Plant-Related Inventions

#### What's Excluded (Section 3(j)):
- Plant varieties
- Seeds
- Plant parts
- Essentially biological processes

#### What May Be Patentable:
| Category | Patentability |
|----------|---------------|
| Isolated plant genes | Possibly (technical application) |
| Plant genetic constructs | Possibly |
| Transformation methods | Yes (technical process) |
| Plant cell lines | Disputed |
| Cultivation methods | If technically inventive |

#### Alternative Protection - PPVFR Act:
- Plant Variety Protection and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001
- Sui generis protection for varieties
- Breeders' rights registration
- Farmers' rights preserved

### Step 5: Biological Diversity Act Compliance

#### Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS):

**Section 6 Requirements**:
| Applicant | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Non-Indian | Prior NBA approval before filing |
| Indian with foreign participation | Prior NBA approval |
| Indian citizen/company | Prior intimation to SBB |

**Application Process**:
1. Identify biological resource used
2. Apply to National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)
3. Obtain approval/permission
4. Disclose in patent application
5. Comply with benefit-sharing conditions

#### Consequences of Non-Compliance:
- Ground for pre-grant opposition (Section 25(1))
- Ground for revocation (Section 64)
- NBA can impose conditions
- Benefit-sharing requirements

### Step 6: Claim Drafting for Biotech

#### Microorganism Claims:
```
"An isolated microorganism [species] having [characteristic],
deposited under accession number [IDA reference],
capable of [function/industrial application]."
```

#### Process Claims:
```
"A method for producing [product] comprising:
(a) culturing [microorganism] under [conditions];
(b) expressing [gene/protein];
(c) isolating [product] by [technique]."
```

#### Gene/Sequence Claims (if permissible):
```
"An isolated nucleic acid molecule comprising:
(a) a nucleotide sequence as set forth in SEQ ID NO: [X]; or
(b) a nucleotide sequence having at least [90]% identity to (a);
wherein said molecule encodes a protein having [function]."
```

### Step 7: Responding to Section 3(j) Objections

#### Strategy Framework:

1. **Narrow to microorganisms**:
   - Focus claims on microbial aspects
   - Avoid plant/animal terminology

2. **Emphasize technical process**:
   - Non-essentially biological
   - Human intervention essential
   - Technical character demonstrated

3. **Distinguish from nature**:
   - Show isolation or modification
   - Technical effect demonstrated
   - Industrial application clear

4. **Alternative claim strategies**:
   - Process claims instead of product
   - Use/application claims
   - Composition claims (formulations)

## Decision Tree: Biotech Patentability

```
Is the invention:
│
├─ Whole plant/animal → NOT PATENTABLE
│
├─ Plant/animal part (seeds, organs) → NOT PATENTABLE
│
├─ Plant variety → NOT PATENTABLE (use PPVFR)
│
├─ Essentially biological process → NOT PATENTABLE
│   (natural reproduction/selection)
│
├─ Microorganism → POTENTIALLY PATENTABLE
│   ├─ Naturally occurring → Limited (need isolation)
│   └─ Modified/isolated → Patentable (with deposit)
│
├─ Technical process for biotech → POTENTIALLY PATENTABLE
│   └─ Human intervention essential
│
├─ Isolated gene/sequence → UNCERTAIN
│   ├─ With technical application → May be patentable
│   └─ Mere discovery → Not patentable
│
└─ Biological material-based product → ANALYZE
    ├─ Not plant/animal part → May be patentable
    └─ Is plant/animal part → NOT PATENTABLE
```

# Protocol: Government Use of Patents (Sections 100-102)

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-026
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Compulsory Mechanisms
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for government use of patented inventions under Sections 100-102 of the Patents Act, including use for government purposes, acquisition of patents, and secrecy directions, with focus on public interest applications.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Government need for patented technology
- National emergency or defense need
- Public health crisis response
- Government procurement decision
- National security considerations
- Secrecy order on patent application

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of government use provisions
- Knowledge of Section 100-102 framework
- Awareness of procedural requirements
- Understanding of compensation mechanisms

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understanding Government Use Framework

#### Three Key Provisions:

| Section | Subject | Purpose |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **100** | Government use of patents | Use without consent |
| **101** | Acquisition of patents | Compulsory purchase |
| **102** | Secrecy directions | National security |

#### Section 100 - Use for Government Purposes:

"Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, at any time after an application for a patent has been filed... any department of the Central Government... may use the invention for the purposes of Government..."

### Step 2: Section 100 - Government Use

#### Scope of Government Use:

**Who Can Use**:
- Central Government departments
- State Governments (with Central authorization)
- Authorized third parties (contractors)

**When**:
- After patent application filed (even before grant)
- At any time during patent term
- Without negotiation with patentee

**For What**:
- "Purposes of Government"
- Includes defense, public utilities
- Public health emergencies
- Any governmental function

#### What "Purposes of Government" Includes:

| Purpose | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Defense | Military equipment production |
| Public health | Epidemic drug production |
| Infrastructure | Public utility equipment |
| Agriculture | Food security technology |
| Education | Research and teaching |

### Step 3: Section 100 Procedure

#### Government Use Authorization:

```
Step 1: Identify patented invention needed
        ↓
Step 2: Government department determines necessity
        ↓
Step 3: Authorization by appropriate authority
        ↓
Step 4: Use commences (may be immediate)
        ↓
Step 5: Intimation to patentee
        ↓
Step 6: Remuneration determination
```

#### Authorization Requirements:
- Written authorization preferred
- Can be retrospective for ongoing use
- No prior negotiation required
- No prior approval from patentee

### Step 4: Remuneration for Government Use

#### Section 100(3) - Compensation:

"[T]he terms [shall be] agreed upon between [Government] and the patentee... or in default of agreement [determined by] the High Court"

#### Remuneration Factors:
1. Scope of use
2. Duration of use
3. Commercial value
4. Investment by patentee
5. Comparable licenses
6. Economic circumstances

#### Determination Process:
```
Negotiation between Government and Patentee
        ↓
    Agreement → Compensation paid
        ↓
    No Agreement → High Court determination
```

### Step 5: Section 101 - Acquisition of Patents

#### Compulsory Acquisition:

**Section 101(1)**:
"Where... the Central Government is satisfied that a patent... should be acquired... for a public purpose, [it] may [acquire] the patent"

#### Grounds for Acquisition:
- National defense
- Public interest
- Food security
- Public health
- Any public purpose

#### Acquisition Procedure:

1. **Central Government Notification**:
   - Published in Official Gazette
   - Specifies patent to be acquired
   - States public purpose

2. **Effect of Acquisition**:
   - Patent vests in Central Government
   - All rights transferred
   - Existing licenses affected

3. **Compensation**:
   - Fair compensation
   - Determined by agreement or arbitration
   - Market value consideration

### Step 6: Section 102 - Secrecy Directions

#### National Security Secrecy:

**When Applied**:
- Defense-related inventions
- Atomic energy matters
- National security concerns

**Procedure**:

```
Patent application filed
        ↓
Controller identifies sensitive subject
        ↓
Reference to Central Government
        ↓
Government directs secrecy (Section 35)
        ↓
Application prohibited from publication
        ↓
Foreign filing restricted (Section 39)
        ↓
Periodic review (Section 36)
```

#### Section 102 Compensation:
- Compensation for loss due to secrecy
- Period of secrecy considered
- Agreement or High Court determination

### Step 7: COVID-19 Context and Recent Applications

#### Government Use During Pandemic:

**Potential Applications**:
- Drug production for epidemic
- Vaccine manufacturing
- Medical device production
- Diagnostic technology

**Section 100 vs. Compulsory Licensing**:

| Aspect | Section 100 | Section 84 CL |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Who uses | Government | Third party |
| Prior negotiation | No | Yes (generally) |
| Controller application | No | Yes |
| Speed | Immediate | Months |
| Compensation | Later determined | Pre-determined |

#### Recommendation for Emergencies:
Section 100 provides faster mechanism for government response than Section 84 compulsory licensing.

---

## Legal Analysis: Government Use Scope

### "Purposes of Government" Interpretation:

**Broad Interpretation** (Preferred):
- All governmental functions
- Public utilities
- Public welfare activities
- Defense and security
- Public health programs

**Narrow Interpretation** (Rejected):
- Only sovereign functions
- Only defense purposes

### Third-Party Authorization:

Section 100(1): "...any person authorized in writing by a Government department"

**Includes**:
- Government contractors
- PSUs (Public Sector Undertakings)
- Authorized manufacturers
- Research institutions

## PROT-IPL-027-national-emergency-section-92.md

# Protocol: National Emergency & Public Health (Section 92)

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-027
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Compulsory Mechanisms
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for compulsory licensing during national emergencies, extreme urgency, and public non-commercial use under Section 92 of the Patents Act, with focus on public health emergencies and pandemic response.

---

## Activation Triggers

- National emergency declaration
- Public health crisis (pandemic, epidemic)
- Extreme urgency circumstances
- Public non-commercial use need
- Essential medicine access crisis

---

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of Section 92 framework
- Distinction from Section 84 CL
- Knowledge of notification requirements
- TRIPS Article 31 awareness

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Understanding Section 92 Framework

#### Section 92 - Special Compulsory Licensing:

"If the Central Government is satisfied... that it is necessary that compulsory licenses should be granted... in circumstances of national emergency or in circumstances of extreme urgency or in case of public non-commercial use"

#### Key Characteristics:

| Feature | Section 92 | Section 84 (Normal CL) |
|---------|------------|------------------------|
| Trigger | Emergency/Urgency | Non-working after 3 years |
| Prior negotiation | Not required | Required (6 months) |
| Waiting period | None | 3 years from grant |
| Government role | Central notification | Application to Controller |
| Speed | Immediate | 6-12 months |

### Step 2: Grounds for Section 92

#### Three Distinct Grounds:

**1. National Emergency**
- War or armed conflict
- Natural disasters
- Pandemic/epidemic
- Economic crisis

**2. Extreme Urgency**
- Public health emergency
- Critical shortage of essential goods
- Imminent threat requiring immediate action

**3. Public Non-Commercial Use**
- Government use without profit motive
- Public health programs
- Research and education
- Public infrastructure

### Step 3: Notification Process

#### Central Government Notification:

```
Health/Industrial Crisis Identified
        ↓
Central Government Assessment
        ↓
Notification in Official Gazette
        ↓
Specific patents identified (optional)
        ↓
Compulsory license applications invited
        ↓
Controller grants license (expedited)
```

#### Notification Content:

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Circumstances | Emergency/urgency described |
| Scope | Product categories or specific patents |
| Duration | Period of notification validity |
| Terms reference | General terms or specific conditions |

### Step 4: Application for Section 92 License

#### Simplified Application:

**What's NOT Required** (Unlike Section 84):
- 3-year waiting period
- 6-month prior negotiation
- Proof of patentee's failure
- Reasonable terms evidence

**What IS Required**:
- Application to Controller
- Statement of capability
- Proposed terms
- Reference to notification

#### Application Format:

```
APPLICATION FOR COMPULSORY LICENSE
UNDER SECTION 92 OF THE PATENTS ACT, 1970

To: Controller of Patents

1. APPLICANT DETAILS
   - Name: [...]
   - Address: [...]
   - Manufacturing capability: [...]

2. PATENT DETAILS
   - Patent No.: [...]
   - Title: [...]
   - Patentee: [...]

3. BASIS FOR APPLICATION
   - Notification reference: [Gazette reference]
   - Ground: [Emergency/Urgency/Public non-commercial]

4. PROPOSED USE
   - Product: [...]
   - Quantity: [...]
   - Market: [India/Export under 92A]

5. PROPOSED TERMS
   - Royalty: [...]
   - Duration: [...]
   - Reporting: [...]

6. DECLARATION
   [Capability and compliance undertaking]
```

### Step 5: Controller's Expedited Procedure

#### Section 92(3) - Fast-Track Grant:

"[T]he Controller shall... grant to the applicant a license under the patent on such terms and conditions as he thinks fit"

#### Timeline:
- No hearing required unless Controller deems necessary
- Expedited processing
- Grant can be immediate

#### Terms Determination:

| Factor | Consideration |
|--------|---------------|
| Royalty | Adequate remuneration (lower than normal) |
| Scope | Limited to emergency purpose |
| Duration | Limited to emergency period |
| Territory | Predominantly India |
| Quantity | As needed for purpose |

### Step 6: Section 92A - Export for Public Health

#### Special Export License:

"Compulsory license shall be available for manufacture and export of patented pharmaceutical products to any country having insufficient or no manufacturing capacity... to address public health problems"

#### Doha Declaration Implementation:

**Eligible Importing Countries**:
- Least developed countries (automatic)
- Countries with public health emergency
- Countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity

**Requirements for Export License**:
- Importing country notification to WTO
- Compulsory license or government use in importing country
- Quantity limited to importing country's need
- Special packaging/labeling required

#### Section 92A Procedure:

```
Importing country declares need to WTO
        ↓
Importing country issues CL/govt authorization
        ↓
Indian manufacturer applies under Section 92A
        ↓
Controller grants export-specific CL
        ↓
Manufacture and export (special labeling)
        ↓
Adequate remuneration to patentee
```

### Step 7: COVID-19 and Pandemic Applications

#### Pandemic Response Toolkit:

| Mechanism | Speed | Best For |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Section 92 CL | Fast | Drug/vaccine manufacture |
| Section 100 | Immediate | Government direct use |
| Section 92A | Medium | Export to other countries |
| Voluntary license | Variable | Cooperative arrangement |

#### COVID-19 Precedent Considerations:

**TRIPS Waiver Discussions**:
- 2020-2022 WTO negotiations
- Ministerial Decision June 2022
- Limited waiver for vaccines

**India's Position**:
- Strong flexibilities exist in law
- Section 92 available for pandemic
- Voluntary licensing preferred (Covaxin)

---

## Section 92 vs. Other Mechanisms

### Comparative Analysis:

| Aspect | Section 84 | Section 92 | Section 100 |
|--------|------------|------------|-------------|
| User | Any person | Any person | Government |
| Trigger | Non-working | Emergency | Govt purpose |
| Waiting | 3 years | None | None |
| Negotiation | Required | Not required | Not required |
| Speed | 6-12 months | Days-weeks | Immediate |
| Controller | Full procedure | Expedited | Not involved |
| Royalty | Reasonable | Adequate | Later |

### When to Use Section 92:

✅ **Use Section 92 when**:
- National emergency declared
- Public health crisis
- Extreme time pressure
- Government notification issued

❌ **Use Section 84 when**:
- Non-working after 3 years
- Price concerns
- Availability issues
- No emergency

# Protocol: Customs Border Enforcement

**Protocol ID**: PROT-IPL-028
**Version**: 1.0.0
**Domain**: Indian Patent Law
**Category**: Enforcement
**Last Updated**: 2026-01-16

---

## Purpose

Comprehensive guide for customs border measures for patent enforcement in India, including IPR (Imported Goods) Rules 2007, recording procedures, detention and seizure mechanisms, and coordination with customs authorities.

---

## Activation Triggers

- Infringing goods being imported
- Counterfeiting detected at border
- Parallel import concerns
- Customs recordation planning
- Border seizure request

---

## Prerequisites

- Granted Indian patent
- Evidence of infringement
- Knowledge of customs procedures
- Understanding of IPR Rules 2007
- Coordination with customs broker

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Legal Framework

#### Intellectual Property Rights (Imported Goods) Enforcement Rules, 2007:

**Authority**: Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue

**Scope**:
- Applies to imported goods
- Covers patents, trademarks, copyrights, designs
- Customs detention and seizure
- Destruction of infringing goods

**Key Provisions**:
- Rule 3: Right holder registration
- Rule 4: Suspension of clearance
- Rule 5: Inspection and examination
- Rule 6: Release or destruction

### Step 2: IPR Recordation with Customs

#### Rule 3 - Notice to Customs:

**Who Can File**:
- Patent owner (patentee)
- Exclusive licensee
- Authorized agent

**What to File (Form IPR-1)**:

| Information | Details Required |
|-------------|------------------|
| Patent details | Number, date, title, claims |
| Ownership | Patentee name, address |
| Authorization | If filed by agent |
| Product description | What's covered by patent |
| Infringing goods | Expected imports |
| Evidence | Samples, images, technical description |
| Ports | Where infringement expected |
| Security | Bank guarantee |

#### Form IPR-1 Components:

1. **Right holder particulars**
2. **IPR details** (patent number, date of grant)
3. **Product description**
4. **Description of suspected infringing goods**
5. **Expected ports of entry**
6. **Indemnity undertaking**
7. **Security/bank guarantee**

### Step 3: Customs Recordation Procedure

#### Filing Process:

```
Prepare Form IPR-1 with supporting documents
        ↓
File with Commissioner of Customs (HQ)
        ↓
Provide security/bank guarantee
        ↓
Customs verifies and accepts recording
        ↓
Information circulated to all ports
        ↓
Recording valid for 5 years (renewable)
```

#### Required Documents:

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Patent certificate | Proof of rights |
| Claim chart | Technical identification |
| Sample products | Genuine vs. infringing |
| Power of attorney | If agent filing |
| Indemnity bond | Protect customs |
| Bank guarantee | Security (typically ₹1-10 lakh) |

#### Validity:
- 5 years from recording
- Renewable
- Can be revoked on patent expiry

### Step 4: Customs Detention (Ex-Officio/On Request)

#### Rule 4 - Suspension of Clearance:

**Ex-Officio Action** (Customs initiated):
- Customs identifies suspected goods
- Notifies right holder
- Right holder must respond within 10 working days

**On Request** (Right holder initiated):
- Right holder files complaint
- Provides prima facie evidence
- Customs detains goods

#### Detention Notice:
```
CUSTOMS DETENTION NOTICE
(Under IPR Rules, 2007)

To: [Importer Name]
    [Right Holder Name]

Bill of Entry No.: [...]
Goods Description: [...]
Quantity: [...]
Origin: [...]

Notice is hereby given that the above goods have been detained
on suspicion of infringing Patent No. IN [...] owned by [...].

Right Holder: Respond within 10 working days
Importer: May provide counter-evidence

Commissioner of Customs
Port: [...]
Date: [...]
```

### Step 5: Examination and Determination

#### Rule 5 - Inspection:

**Right Holder Rights**:
- Inspect detained goods
- Take samples (with approval)
- Provide technical analysis
- Submit expert opinion

**Importer Rights**:
- Respond to infringement claim
- Provide evidence of authorization
- Challenge patent validity
- Request release

#### Technical Examination:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Customs shares product details |
| 2 | Right holder examines samples |
| 3 | Technical comparison report |
| 4 | Claims mapping |
| 5 | Infringement determination |

### Step 6: Disposition of Goods

#### Rule 6 - Release or Destruction:

**Outcome Options**:

| Scenario | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Infringement confirmed | Destruction or re-export |
| No infringement | Release to importer |
| Right holder fails to act | Release after 10 days |
| Litigation pending | Hold pending court order |

#### Destruction Procedure:
1. Infringement confirmed
2. Importer given opportunity to be heard
3. Destruction order issued
4. Goods destroyed under supervision
5. Certificate of destruction issued

### Step 7: Coordination with Litigation

#### Customs Action + Court Action:

**Strategy**:
```
Customs detention (border)
        +
Civil suit for infringement (court)
        +
Interim injunction application
        =
Comprehensive enforcement
```

#### Court Involvement:
- Customs detention is administrative
- Court order for permanent remedy
- Anton Piller orders for evidence
- Injunction to prevent future imports

```

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│   Right Holder  │   Right Holder  │
```
